# Magic System

Compiled from a full sequential read of Draft Zero. The author's own glossary (`manuscript/glossary.md`)
defines the core terms verbatim; this document adds how those terms are shown functioning in the
narrative, chapter by chapter, plus the pantheon and factional structure the magic system sits inside.

## Two systems: Arcane and Profane

- **Arcane** — Eyael's "orthodox" Magic system (glossary). Practitioners are **Arcanists**: people
  born with a soul attuned to the Arcane. Arcanists can sense each other's presence as a disturbance
  in ambient **Aether** (Edward's presence "stirred the air," Ch1; Carden senses Renee's Zeal Magic
  in the crowd, Ch2).
- **Profane** — Eyael's "demonic, unorthodox" counterpart system (glossary), practiced by
  **Profaners**. Not shown in active use on-page in the read chapters; referenced mainly in passing
  curses ("Profane-infested hells," Ch4) and in the existence of **Profane Creatures** and cyclical
  **Profane Floods** (glossary).
- **Aether** is the shared substrate: "the primordial invisible energy that permeates Eyael... the
  environmental medium necessary to manifest Magical powers" (glossary). Both Magic systems draw on
  it; it is also the raw material **Worldhearts** convert into Aetheric Creatures, Arcane Creatures,
  Beastmen, and natural resources via **Spawning** (glossary).

## Arcane Tongue
- A "mystically inviolable language" that only Arcanists can speak or understand, and in which only
  truth can be spoken (established explicitly Ch1: "Arcane Tongue only allowed one to speak and write
  the truth"). Used repeatedly as a truth-forcing register:
  - The God of War's and the Creator's *Scriptures* are written/spoken in it, which is explicitly
    cited as self-authenticating proof of their divine origin (Ch1, Ch5).
  - Horace's Law Magic interrogation of the riot suspects forces truth-telling in it; the two
    non-Arcanist suspects (an Elf slave and her Human husband) visibly suffer when attempting to lie,
    while the Arcanist daughter does not flinch from confessing (Ch3) — implying fluency/attunement
    determines whether the compulsion can be resisted or merely whether lying causes pain.
  - Carden and Julian's Ch5 confrontation is conducted entirely in Arcane Tongue specifically so each
    forces the other to speak truth ("the fact he spoke in Arcane Tongue indicated he wanted nothing
    but truth from Carden").
  - Carden's resignation speech to Edward (Ch7) is delivered in Arcane Tongue as a deliberate signal
    of sincerity.
- Functions as an in-group marker among Arcanists for passing information civilians/Profaners should
  not have (Carden and Roger flashing wrist-tattoos to a regimental commander to establish
  credentials, Ch3; Carden telling Roger classified information in Arcane Tongue, Ch8).

## Named Magic "Branches" seen on-page
The text names **Eighteen Magic Branches** (symbolized by the Creator's
eighteen-branched tree icon, Ch4) but does not enumerate them. Branches/disciplines directly named
or shown in use in the read chapters:

| Branch (as named in text) | Shown doing | Wielded by |
|---|---|---|
| Zeal Magic | Amplifies the emotional/persuasive force of speech; can intimidate (interrogating Laplace, Ch2), rouse a hostile crowd into pausing (Ch2 riot), undercut an opponent's confidence in an argument (Carden vs. Julian, Ch5), or encourage/sustain laborers (Epilogue). | Carden Oakmoore, Renee Rose (both "Ideals Arcanists") |
| Law Magic | Compels true confession via the single Arcane-Tongue command "Confess"; also has a detection/sensing capability ("My Law Magic just confirmed we found our suspects," Ch3) | Horace Livingstone |
| Armaments Magic | Manifests Magical armor and weapons (dusk-orange full plate armor and broadsword). Feroz Surena's investiture sword shares the same dusk-orange coloring (Ch1), suggesting the same Branch. | Julian Oakmoore (Ch5); possibly Feroz Surena (Ch1) |
| Space Magic | Instant teleportation, with a characteristic "rumbling thud" | Sarhese naval vessels (Ch3) |
| Gravitational Magic | Propels vessels silently without visible engines | Sarhese fleet (Ch3) |
| Atmospheric Magic | Conjures storm-cover at supernatural speed and scale ("the work of Imperial Demigods") | Imperial forces (Ch3, Ch5) |
| Shadow Magic | A ring-shaped Magic Artifact grants entry to the formless "Shadow World," a pocket space viewed as a dark mirror beneath the real one | Julian Oakmoore (via artifact, Ch5) |
| Mitotic Magic | Rapid wound-healing (closes a deep neck cut in about a minute) | An enslaved Elf healer at the Exchequer infirmary (Ch5) |
| Empowerment Magic / Enchanting Magic | Used together in construction labor: orange-haired Elf slaves crawl onto the lattice and with their Empowerment Magic gracefully catch large tempered-glass panes tossed up by their compatriots; sky-blue-haired Elf slaves then seal the panes onto the lattice with their conventional tools and generous application of Enchanting Magic. The lattice itself is hoisted by a cranking, biofuel-engine-powered crane. | Enslaved Elves (Epilogue) |

## Tiers, Callings, and Self-Actualization
- **Tier** — discrete power levels applying to Arcanists, Profaners, and all Aether-derived creature
  types alike (glossary).
- **Calling** — the specific role/profession an Arcanist or Profaner occupies at a given Magic Branch
  and Tier, defining their available powers and the "Calling-specific feat" they must perform to
  progress (glossary). Carden and Renee share the Calling "Ideals Arcanist."
- **Self-Actualization** — advancement is not grinding or ritual but existential: the practitioner
  must "embody and live the ideals of their Calling" (glossary) — thematically mirrored by the
  Epilogue's title, "Magic Is Self-Actualization," and by Carden's own arc, in which his power
  (Zeal Magic persuasion/conviction) only strengthens once his convictions and actions align (his
  resignation on principle, Ch7, followed immediately by visibly greater confidence/competence
  wielding Zeal Magic in the Epilogue). In the Epilogue, Carden explicitly hears his Zealot
  Calling's whisper: "*Stir the masses into action. Unite them behind a better future.*" His
  spirit trembles as if a breakthrough lingers just a hairsbreadth away, yet he cannot identify the
  missing piece — self-actualization is shown as imminent but not yet achieved on-page.
- **Demigod** — an Arcanist or Profaner who has reached a Tier high enough to attain divinity
  (glossary). Alfainia maintains resident Demigods who sit in special Parliament balconies (Ch1);
  a crushing wave of divinity during the Ch1 investiture staggers every Demigod present
  simultaneously — Carden rules out any single Demigod as the source ("One Demigod, no matter how
  strong, could not simultaneously restrain so many of his peers"), identifying the true actor as
  the God of War Himself manifesting His presence directly.

## Peoples (non-human)
Per the glossary, layered by rarity/constituency of Eyael's ecosystems:
- **Beastmen** — sapient but primal; smallest ecosystem constituent.
- **Arcane Creatures** — feral; minor constituent.
- **Aetheric Creatures** — feral, elementally empowered at higher Tiers; main constituent.
- **Profane Creatures** — feral, spawned specifically during Profane Floods; an invasive/aberrant
  category.
- **Essence** (in Arcane/Profane Creatures and Beastmen) and **Magic Gland** (in Arcane Creatures)
  are the biological organs that store/cast innate Magic and can be harvested to forge **Magic
  Artifacts** or consumed in a single-use **Magic Charm** (glossary).

Sapient peoples shown on-page: **Humans**, **Dwarves** (short, unpigmented, subterranean-adapted),
and **Elves** (human-sized, pointed ears, grey skin, hair/eye color tied to innate Magical affinity).
Elves appear exclusively in enslaved roles in the read chapters — a captured rioter's enslaved Elf
mother bears a visible slave-tattoo on her wrist (Ch3), an Elf healer at the Exchequer is referred
to as a "slave" (Ch5), and
affluent Windbury families are shown loaning out Elf slaves for reconstruction labor in the Epilogue
— a background social detail the text notes but does not yet interrogate directly.

## Pantheon and the war's theology
- **The Father** — deity of Carden's (and, formerly, the Winstons') faith; associated with love as
  "the greatest commandment" (Ch7) and with Saint Alfred/the Essexian Church. His *Scripture*,
  delivered via Saint Alfred's cathedral, speaks of the Father's loving provision and care ("What
  father when his son asks for bread gives him a snake?", Ch7). The priest's sermon at the same
  service teaches "resilience amidst intense persecution."
- **The God of War** — deity backing Sarh and, via the 74-year-old liberation-era compact, Alfainia's
  government; His *Scripture* (Ch1) explicitly calls for war against the Creator and "his empire."
  His divine presence manifests at the Ch1 investiture (a crushing force of divinity, not a physical
  form) — an unprecedented deviation from ritual that the text treats as a portent of the coming war.
- **The Creator** — deity of Imperium Divinus (the former occupying empire); His *Scripture*
  (mysteriously appearing in Parliament with no traceable origin, Ch5) claims sole legitimacy over
  all government, religion, and peace, and threatens "Sacred War" against those who don't submit.
  Secretly still worshipped inside Alfainia by Imperial-descended loyalists (the Oakmoores, and later
  the Winstons) who "eagerly yearn for Alfainia's return to the Creator's empire" (Ch4).
- The war brewing across the novella is framed, repeatedly and explicitly (Ch1, Ch5), as a
  centuries-old feud between the God of War and the Creator, with Alfainia and its Dominions cast as
  expendable instruments ("cogs," Ch5) of that divine rivalry rather than genuine participants in
  it — the novella's central political/thematic tension.
- **Saint Alfred** — Alfainia's founding liberator, "more than a hundred years" distant from the old
  Imperium's original fall (per Charles Winston's looser framing, Ch4) and specifically the figure
  who, "fifty, sixty years ago," led/witnessed Alfainia's liberation from a nine-year Imperial
  occupation before vanishing roughly five decades ago, reportedly in protest of Alfainia turning to
  the God of War for that liberation (Ch7). Possibly still alive and active incognito — see
  `characters.md` → "Alfred."

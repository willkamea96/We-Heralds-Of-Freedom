# Narrative Structure and Plot

Compiled from a full, sequential, chapter-by-chapter read of the Draft Zero manuscript (`manuscript/front-matter.md`,
`chapter-01.md` … `chapter-08.md`, `epilogue.md`, `glossary.md`). This document covers **only** the manuscript's
narrative structure (how the story is built and ordered) and its plot (what happens, in order). Character profiles,
the magic system, locations, and glossary terms live in the other story-bible files and appear here only where they
are needed to follow an event.

**Citation format.** `(ch04:66–138)` means lines 66–138 of `manuscript/chapter-04.md` (line numbers as shown by a
Markdown viewer, blank lines included). `ep` = `manuscript/epilogue.md`. Where the manuscript is silent, this
document says so rather than inferring; where an event is only implied, it is labelled "implied".

- Eight numbered chapters plus a one-scene epilogue. The manuscript uses no part/act divisions, no chapter epigraphs,
  and no section headings inside chapters; scene changes inside a chapter are signalled only by blank-line breaks and
  by the opening sentence of the new scene.
- Chapter lengths (words, from `manuscript/INDEX.md`): Ch1 3,232 · Ch2 4,224 · Ch3 2,010 · Ch4 3,871 · Ch5 3,826 ·
  Ch6 3,025 · Ch7 3,708 · Ch8 2,351 · Epilogue 850. Chapter Three is the shortest numbered chapter; Chapter Two is the longest.
- The chapter titles of Chapters Seven and Eight form a deliberate pair ("When Faith Is Tested" / "When Faith Is
  Rewarded"), and the Epilogue title ("Magic Is Self-Actualization") is the only title that names the story's magic concept.

### 1.2 Narration and point of view

- **Close third person, past tense, fixed on one focal character: Carden Oakmoore.** Every scene is one in which Carden
  is present; his perceptions, memories, and opinions colour the narration (e.g. ch01:12, ch01:48, ch04:196).
- **No other character is ever a POV character.** Marelyn Winston, Horace, Edward, Annie, and Roger are all seen only
  from outside, through Carden's observation and guesses (his frequent mis-readings of Marelyn are part of the
  structure — e.g. ch07:255–265, where he assumes she has won alimony).
- **Off-page events are relayed through dialogue, never dramatized.** Examples: Marelyn's information-gathering from her
  father, brother, officers' wives and friends (ch05:218); Marelyn's dinner with Roger (ch06:88–92); Horace's meeting
  and audit with Edward (ch06:196–200); Edward notifying Julian and Charles (ch07:42); Marelyn's lunch with both fathers
  (ch07:56–62); Marelyn's day of selling her purses and filing the disinheritance (ch07:277–291).
- **Italic thought-lines** interrupt the narration at emotional turns (e.g. "*Oh no*." ch01:152; "*Of course she found
  someone else.*" ch02:106; "*Drama is the last thing I want right now.*" ch04:10; "*I guess this is the end of our
  marriage. We had a good run.*" ch07:171).
- **Intrusive war memories** appear as short, present-feeling flashes inside scenes rather than as flashback scenes:
  ch01:86 (blackened artillery-blasted fields), ch01:144 (amphibious assault on a coastal city), ch03:116 (a slack wrist
  with a black-hound tattoo), ch03:132 (black airships), ch05:210 (corpses blasted over endless black fields).
- **Arcane Tongue (truth-only language) marks the moments of sincerity or forced honesty:** the opening ritual exchange
  (ch01:20), the Scripture readings (ch01:134–138, ch05:86–96), Carden's statement to the half-Elf woman (ch02:308–310),
  Horace's "*Confess*." (ch03:66), the Carden–Julian confrontation (ch05:144–196), Carden's answer to Edward (ch07:191–201),
  Carden's warning to Roger about Horace (ch08:54), and Renee's thanks to Carden (ep:22).
- **Embedded documents break up the prose:** two telegrams (ch03:4–12, ch03:166–172), three *Scriptures* (ch01:134–138,
  ch05:86–96, ch07:126–129), newspaper headlines (ch05:10–16), and the exemption/termination/disinheritance/divorce
  paperwork that physically moves through Chapters Six to Eight.

### 1.3 Chronological handling

- **Strictly linear.** There are no flashback scenes and no jumps to a different time-line; the story moves forward from
  the investiture to the Epilogue.
- **Chapters are mostly continuous with their neighbours.** Chapter Three ends on the morning after Chapter Two's riot;
  Chapter Four is the same weekend (Julian refers to "yesterday's Imperial raid", ch04:110; Beatrice to the air-raid
  "yesterday", ch04:156–158); Chapter Six opens by continuing the last line of Chapter Five; Chapter Seven opens by
  continuing the last line of Chapter Six.
- **Explicit time-skips (all stated in the text):**

| Between | Gap stated or implied in the text |
|---------|-----------------------------------|
| Ch1 → Ch2 | About three months (Edward's tax hike is "three months ago" at ch02:48; Carden's "past three months of stress" ch02:88). Ch2 is set on a winter morning (ch02:6) in the week of Deliverance Day (ch02:176). |
| Ch2 → Ch3 → Ch4 | None: Ch2's riot (Deliverance Day, Saturday) → Ch3 (that evening, night, and next morning) → Ch4 (the Sunday). |
| Ch4 → Ch5 | To "spring's first buds" (ch05:4); Carden has walked Marelyn to the Stock Exchange for "three months now" (ch05:24). |
| Within Ch5 | "A few days later" tax hike; then "Three months after Carden and Marelyn made their private vow" the first Dominion incursion; summer arrives (ch05:278–282). |
| Ch5 → Ch6 | Ch5 ends as summer sweeps in (ch05:282). The Saint Alfred's thanksgiving visit in Ch6 (ch06:138–166) is later recalled as "the turn of summer" (ch07:82). |
| Within Ch6 | "Months went by"; summer ends, fall arrives, the rearmament parade (ch06:168–170); then "the middle of autumn" (ch06:182). |

| Ch8 → Epilogue | Winter; the Epilogue refers to "the welfare-for-volunteering program autumn last year" (ep:16); construction has been in progress for three months (ep:12). |

### 1.4 Movements by setting (a descriptive grouping, not an author-labelled act structure)

The manuscript never labels acts. Grouped by where the action happens, the story alternates between the capital and
Windbury:

1. **Portsmouth** — Chapter One; first half of Chapter Two (investiture, God of War's *Scripture*, Laplace, Horace, Bakers' petition).
2. **Windbury** — second half of Chapter Two, Chapter Three, Chapter Four (Deliverance Day riot, martial law, Imperial flotilla, parents' houses, rings, approval of the Bakers' exemption). Chapter Four ends with the return to Portsmouth.
3. **Portsmouth** — Chapters Five, Six, Seven (escalating rearmament, second petition, ultimatum, termination, eviction).
4. **Windbury** — Chapter Eight and the Epilogue (return, rebuilding proposal, volunteer reconstruction).

### 1.6 The three *Scripture* encounters (a recurring structural device)

Divine documents, each in Arcane Tongue, are physically read by Carden at three points, in this order:

1. **The God of War's *Scripture*** — Edward hands it to Carden at the Commonwealth Exchequer (ch01:132–140). Edward also shows it to Roger and Annie (ch02:158).
2. **The Creator's *Scripture*** — a pile of them appeared in Parliament "two nights ago"; Edward has Carden touch one (ch05:78–98).
3. **The Father's *Scripture*** — read aloud in the Saint Alfred's Cathedral sermon and then touched by Carden at the altar (ch07:82–129).

In the first two, Edward puts the document in front of Carden in an office scene; in the third, Carden himself asks the cathedral priest "May I?" and
touches the scroll at the altar. The god-of-war and Creator documents command or threaten war; the Father's document is a short passage about parents and children (ch07:126–129).

## Part 2 — Backstory the narrative reveals (events before Chapter One)

Only facts the text states. Source citations are to where the narrative reveals them.

- **Alfainia's liberation and the compact.** Alfainia was liberated "seventy-four-years ago" (ch01:52); the occupation is Imperium Divinus's, and it lasted nine years (ch05:128). The Alfainia–Sarh (God of War) friendship is renewed at each investiture (ch01:50). The founding-era Saint Alfred is described by Charles Winston as having freed the nation "from the old Imperium's rot more than a hundred years ago" (ch04:168); Saint Alfred "vanished from the face of the world more than five-decades ago during Alfainia's liberation from Imperial occupation", ostensibly because he felt betrayed that Alfainia turned to the God of War for its freedom (ch07:133).
- **The Oakmoore parents.** Julian and Jane were Imperial colonists who stayed after the liberation but never abandoned their faith (ch04:52). Carden says that when Imperium Divinus abandoned them they were left with nothing and built a business empire provisioning Alfainia's military (ch05:158). They have spent about twenty-five years converting every member of the Winston family to the Creator's faith (ch04:102–104).
- **The arranged marriage.** Carden and Marelyn have been married twenty-five years (ch01:210) in a marriage arranged by their parents (ch01:190, 212). Carden says Julian forced the marriage (ch05:188). Marelyn tells Julian and Jane she has managed the couple's money; Carden says she has managed it for the full twenty-five years (ch04:86).
- **Carden and Annie.** As teenagers in Windbury they roamed the meadows (ch02:108); they made a promise in Windbury Cathedral to marry once Carden returned from the army (ch06:144); Carden thinks of it as a "broken promise" (ch02:182). Annie later married Roger (ch02:100). Carden and Annie attended Windbury high school with their classmates (ch02:142, ch03:104).
- **Carden's military career.** Several combat tours on the mainland before the marriage (ch01:84); the 309th Infantry Regiment, 8th Infantry Division, "the Black Hounds" (ch01:118–120); his specialty was pacifying occupied enemy settlements (ch01:124). Horace also served with the Black Hounds (ch02:52–56); Roger served with the Fifth Marine Regiment, "the Blue Kingfishers" (ch02:164). Carden and Horace remember "what we suffered back on the mainland" (ch02:56).
- **Carden's career since.** A civil servant for "more than twenty years", "almost half his life" (ch07:205); appointed Special Commissioner for Income Tax (ch01:92); has "four years" ahead under Edward Cook (ch01:124, 166).
- **Carden's last visit home.** Julian says it has been six years since Carden's last visit (ch04:70).
- **Countryside decline.** An elderly woman on the train says the decline in the countryside has been getting worse "the past eight years" regardless of governing party (ch02:262–266).

## Part 3 — Plot, chapter by chapter and scene by scene

### Chapter One – Our Definitions Of Freedom

*Place:* Portsmouth. *Time:* a single day — the investiture ceremony, the afternoon meeting with Edward, and an evening that ends near midnight.

1. **The ritual begins** (ch01:4–10). In Alfainia's Parliament Chamber a Dwarf delegate and the Human delegates perform the ancient call-and-response between Mankind ("Heralds of Freedom") and Dwarfkind ("Pillars of Iron") that opens the recurrent investiture ceremony.
2. **Carden and Marelyn in the gallery** (ch01:12). Carden stifles a yawn and Marelyn elbows him; he thinks that he wishes his wife showed as much regard for their marriage.
3. **The oath exchange** (ch01:14–28). Dwarf delegate Feroz Surena and Alfainia's newly elected Prime Minister, Terence Howell, meet at the centre of the chamber and exchange formal questions and answers in Arcane Tongue (ch01:14–20). Carden dislikes Terence's nervous tic and thinks the Prime Minister commands "the most bloated parliamentary coalition in recent history" (ch01:18, 22). Carden asks Marelyn for her family's view of the cabinet; she says nothing; they voted for different parties and neither pick made the ruling coalition (ch01:26–28).
4. **Kneeling and the sword** (ch01:30–32). Feroz, "Lord Magi over Alfainia" by the authority of the God of War, has Terence kneel, claps his hands, and summons an ephemeral sword of dusk-coloured light as he acknowledges Terence as Prime Minister.
5. **The unscheduled divine presence** (ch01:34–48). As Feroz waves the sword overhead, a terrible force slams Carden down; everyone from the audience above to the dignitaries below stirs, the Dwarves slide off their seats and prostrate, the Humans drop to their knees, and Carden steadies Marelyn's trembling hand and leads her down to her knees (ch01:34). Carden recognizes that the ritual forbids any expression of divinity and asks why Alfainia's Demigods do nothing (ch01:36). The pressure vanishes; the Demigods in the balconies are pale and staggering (ch01:38). Carden reasons that no single Demigod could have restrained so many peers, so the force was not Feroz's (ch01:40); he thinks "Oh no." (ch01:42). Feroz hails the God of War and the Dwarves answer "Hail!" (ch01:44–46). Carden thinks that this "most bloated, pathetic ruling coalition in recent history" has been "honored by the God of War's uninvited personal presence" and hopes he still has a job in the civil service (ch01:48).
6. **The acknowledgement** (ch01:50–58). Feroz taps Terence's shoulders with the orange sword, declares that the God of War "acknowledges your election", and wishes continued friendship between Alfainia and Sarh. Carden thinks this is the first time in the ritual's history that the God of War's presence has manifested and wonders if it is a reminder of whom Alfainia owes its liberation seventy-four years ago (ch01:52). The cabinet kneels and is haloed by the sword; they rise, salute with right fists over their hearts, and pledge allegiance and faith to the God of War in Arcane Tongue, a departure from the ritual where everyday speech normally suffices (ch01:54–58).
7. **"Traitors"** (ch01:60–74). Marelyn sniffs and whispers "Traitors." Carden covers for her aloud with a line about afternoon tea; Marelyn plays along (ch01:66–68). She then whispers: "We used to believe in something. We used to believe in the Father. This is who we're electing now." Carden gives no answer (ch01:72–74).
8. **Reception and the balcony** (ch01:76–100). During post-ceremony refreshments Carden endures flattery about the honour; he leads Marelyn to a balcony over the Rorsch River and asks if she would prefer to leave (ch01:76–80). Marelyn asks why a veteran of several combat tours said nothing (ch01:84). A flash of artillery-blasted fields interrupts Carden; he answers, "A good soldier follows orders." (ch01:86–88). Marelyn challenges: the distinguished veteran appointed Special Commissioner for Income Tax shrugs off "a betrayal of founding principles" by citing orders (ch01:92). Carden says he would gladly resign if she would stay happy as his wife without his salary and benefits; no answer comes but the wind (ch01:96–98). Marelyn says, "I think I'm ready to leave, Carden." (ch01:100).
9. **Alone** (ch01:102–104). Carden puts Marelyn in a carriage, asks the chauffeur to take her home, lights a cigar, and enjoys a moment of peace on the curb.
10. **Edward Cook's office** (ch01:106–164). The new Finance Minister and Trade Party head, Edward Cook, summons Carden to the Commonwealth Exchequer and says "We have been given a mandate by the God of War" (ch01:106). Carden senses Edward is also an Arcanist (ch01:110). Asked about his unit, Carden says the 309th Infantry Regiment, 8th Infantry Division; Edward says "The Black Hounds. So you're an Arcanist from that regiment, interesting. Your files omitted that." (ch01:116–120). Carden says he specialized in pacifying occupied enemy settlements and that the civil service perhaps omitted that "since I do set people's taxes" (ch01:124). Edward slides a stiff document across; from a touch Carden knows right away who wrote it, and a disembodied male voice rumbles in Arcane Tongue above him (ch01:132). The text: the board is almost set, the final pieces are moving, the "world war our peoples long awaited" will be unleashed; Alfainia is to "Raise your armies, commission your fleets" because "The dawn when we topple the Creator and his empire comes." (ch01:134–138). Carden sets the letter down — in his thought, "no, the God of War's *Scripture*" — and hopes Edward missed how his hand trembled; because Arcane Tongue only allows one to speak and write the truth, the document is authenticated (ch01:140). Carden recalls amphibious landings against Imperial Legionaries, with Demigods and Angels duelling overhead (ch01:144). He objects that the cabinet was elected on improving working-class conditions and asks about timeline and public reaction; he thinks the people's opinion matters nothing and no timeline exists (ch01:146–148). Edward says that is where Carden comes in: Terence and the Labor Party will manage public relations, while "we here in Customs and Revenue will scrape the barrel to the nibs"; Edward will forward Parliament a corporate tax hike "in the coming days", and Carden is to turn away entrepreneurs begging for exemptions (ch01:150–158). Carden answers "Yes, sir." and is dismissed (ch01:160–164).
11. **Wheatfield's Bistro** (ch01:166–168). Carden's usual indulgence fails to cheer him; he dreads four years under Edward, and the thought that the God of War's world war might liberate him first.
12. **Home** (ch01:170–212). Near midnight Carden finds Marelyn asleep over financial documents, with no meal. He cooks duck bruschetta (ch01:170–172). She wakes, is startled by the food (ch01:174–176), and asks about the "hiding" (ch01:186–188). He tells her the government received a *Scripture* ordering Alfainia to prepare for a world war (ch01:192). Marelyn concludes that Labor is already breaking its campaign promise and he will enforce the higher taxes (ch01:194). She says his insider knowledge will let her optimise their portfolio, then asks what he thinks (ch01:198). Carden answers that the God of War and his "lackeys in Parliament" are "dangling the illusion of choice but dictate the options" and that Alfainia is being used as a forward-operating base and self-funding arsenal for conquering the Creator's remaining mainland territory (ch01:204). They sit in companionable silence; Marelyn asks "Why can't we make this work, Carden?"; he answers, "Because our parents never gave us a choice." (ch01:206–212).

**Chapter outcome.** The investiture is disrupted by the God of War's presence; Carden is told the war is coming and that he will enforce the tax hike; the evening ends with Marelyn asking "Why can't we make this work, Carden?" and Carden answering "Because our parents never gave us a choice."

### Chapter Two – Home Is Not Home

*Place:* Portsmouth (Carden's office, Edward's office, the flat), then a four-hour train ride to Windbury, then Windbury on Deliverance Day. *Time:* about three months after Chapter One; a winter weekday, then the Saturday.

1. **Laplace** (ch02:4–48). A furniture-firm owner, Laplace, begs a tax exemption (ch02:4–16). Carden notes the firm is non-strategic, has grown profits for three years, and has thriving Dominion exports. Laplace says he grew profits by cutting staff while revenue declined for three years (ch02:20), contradicting his books; Carden realises he is either lying or confessing to falsified accounting (ch02:22–26). Carden uses his Arcanist power, infusing his voice with Zeal Magic, to magnify Laplace's doubt (ch02:30–38). Two tax officers burst in, arrest Laplace, and Carden orders an audit of three years of possible underreported taxes (ch02:34–42). He reflects that three firms this morning are at risk of insolvency or downsizing because of Edward's tax hike three months ago (ch02:48).
2. **Horace's visit** (ch02:50–86). Horace Livingstone enters; his sleeve shows the black hound tattoo matching Carden's (ch02:52–54). Carden and Horace agree the capital is preferable to the mainland (ch02:56–58). They toast with apple cider to Horace's election as Mayor of Windbury (ch02:60–64). Horace says he ran into Marelyn at the Stock Exchange, took her to brunch, and bought her a new purse (ch02:68–76). Horace also says he counselled Marelyn that a woman of her disposition would do more good pouring her energy into her home, that she and Carden are "much deprived without children to care for", and that she "wholeheartedly agreed and asked I air these concerns with you" (ch02:80). Carden understands this means Marelyn wants him to rebuke Horace (ch02:82). Carden tells Horace not to be so friendly with other people's spouses and to keep marital advice to himself (Horace is thrice divorced), then steers to Windbury (ch02:84–86).

6. **Months pass; the parade** (ch06:168–180). Months go by with more Dominion incursions reported (ch06:168). As fall begins, Alfainia holds a festivity in Portsmouth Harbor for the maiden voyage of the first fruits of its rearmament (ch06:170). Marelyn says "So this is where all our money went."; neighbors glare; Carden says government has to show something for its efforts (ch06:172–176). Aerial warships, squadrons, and the naval flotilla pass in review (ch06:178). Some young men cheer but most spectators look sober and weary (ch06:180).
7. **Horace's warning** (ch06:182–212). In the middle of autumn Carden finds Horace in his office (ch06:182). Horace says "Water's fine" and "Horace blinked at the water and then said, 'Apologies, I suppose.'" (ch06:186–190). Horace says he met Edward yesterday to ask for central funding for Windbury and that Edward audited Windbury's finances and is furious about the tax exemptions (ch06:196–200). He says Carden should have come to him first so he could have been careful how he approached Edward (ch06:202). Carden recalls the shrouded Windbury Square and wonders why he never told Horace (ch06:204). Horace says "May the Father be with you, old friend." (ch06:208). Edward's secretary arrives: "Mister Oakmoore. Mister Cook would like to see you." (ch06:210–212).

**Chapter outcome.** Carden approves the second set of exemptions on Marelyn's advice, Edward initially signs them, then Horace reveals that Edward's audit has turned against him.

### Chapter Seven – When Faith Is Tested

*Place:* Portsmouth (Edward's office, the flat, Saint Alfred's Cathedral, the Exchequer, Wheatfield's Bistro). *Time:* a day of ultimatum, the night, the next morning and day.

1. **Edward's ultimatum** (ch07:4–46). Edward says Carden can choose: annul the exemptions and personally collect the past year's arrears, or be terminated (ch07:4–14). Carden asks whether the exemptions stand if he chooses termination; Edward says he will annul them regardless and the question is whether he can still count on Carden as a subordinate (ch07:18–20). Edward says he is offering this because Carden's father is an important partner of government, because his father-in-law is a respected member of Parliament, and because he will not deny "a proven patriot his conscience" (ch07:30). Carden asks for another way; Edward asks what he is supposed to do as Finance Minister while Imperium Divinus challenges the Commonwealth's sovereignty (ch07:32–34). Carden is given until tomorrow morning (ch07:38–40). Edward says he notified Carden's father and father-in-law this morning and they promised the matter will be settled (ch07:42). "I pray you make the right decision, Carden." (ch07:46).
2. **The flat** (ch07:48–76). Carden walks home in an unusually early afternoon (ch07:48). Marelyn is in her study, staring at her wall of purses, clutching crumpled papers (ch07:50). Carden sits on the floor and lays the six tax-exemptions and the unsigned termination letter between them (ch07:52). Marelyn says both fathers sought her out and a chauffeur took her from the Stock Exchange to Julian's Portsmouth penthouse for lunch (ch07:56). She lays an unsigned divorce form and an unsigned disinheritance form with Carden's papers (ch07:58). The families' message: "There is no room for a disgraced prodigal son." (ch07:60–62). Carden asks "What about you?" She asks for the night alone (ch07:66–70). He sets up the spare mattress in the living room and goes out (ch07:74–76).
3. **Saint Alfred's Cathedral** (ch07:78–146). Carden walks across the Rorsch River's bridge into Northern Portsmouth and finds himself at Saint Alfred's Cathedral (ch07:78). The pews hold managers, lawyers, laborers, seamstresses, midwives, and housewives, listening silently to a priest read from a scroll thrumming with divinity (ch07:82–84). Carden sits beside a bearded man in workman's overalls clutching an empty alms bowl; Carden gives him coins (ch07:88). The man asks his name and, after Carden gives it, touches the back of Carden's head and blesses him (ch07:92–98). Carden asks the man's name; he whispers "Alfred." (ch07:102–106). The sermon is about resilience amid persecution; the crowd listens without fear or anger (ch07:110–112). Afterwards Carden asks the old priest if he may touch the *Scripture* (ch07:116–118); no force strikes him; a thin male voice says "What father when his son asks for bread gives him a snake? What mother when her daughter asks for counsel gives her rebuke? If even your parents in this world know what is good for you, how much more your Father above who sees all?" (ch07:124–129). Carden asks if Saint Alfred wrote it; the priest says "Alfred Essex was but the humble messenger. He received this directly from the Father and bequeathed it to us when this cathedral was first built." (ch07:133–135). Carden nearly faints; something clicks (ch07:137). He bows in the Essexian Church's rite and prays "Father in Heaven, please provision Marelyn and I for what comes." (ch07:139–141). Outside, the statue's beard and alms bowl look "impeccably familiar" (ch07:143–145).
4. **The decision** (ch07:147–173). At midnight Carden returns, lights the hearth, and tells Marelyn he went to Saint Alfred's Cathedral (ch07:147–151). Asked his decision, he says "Termination." (ch07:157). Marelyn says "I see." and sobs (ch07:159–163). Carden asks what she thinks; she says "I'm sorry, Carden. I'm so sorry. I just can't. I just can't right now." and locks herself in her study (ch07:165–169). Carden thinks the marriage is over; he sleeps on the spare mattress while she sobs through the night (ch07:171–173).
5. **Termination** (ch07:175–203). Marelyn does not answer the next morning; Carden leaves breakfast (ch07:175). In Edward's office, Carden stands in sunlight while Edward sits in shade (ch07:177–179). He submits the signed termination letter; Edward signs (ch07:183–185). Edward asks for the public record why a civil servant would undermine Parliament's interests (ch07:189). In Arcane Tongue Carden says he believes in the Father, who teaches that love is the greatest commandment, and that he gave his blood for the country Saint Alfred believed in (ch07:191–197). Edward, in Arcane Tongue, thanks him for his service and wishes him a deserved rest: "You are dismissed." (ch07:199–203).
6. **Leaving the Exchequer** (ch07:205–215). After more than twenty years, Carden leaves with his belongings in a box and feels "Free." (ch07:205–207). Chef Wheatfield gives him a free lunch but refuses future discounts (ch07:209). Personnel gives him two days to vacate the flat (ch07:213).
7. **The empty flat** (ch07:217–247). Carden finds the flat spotless, the knickknacks and accessories that marked his and Marelyn's life together gone (ch07:217); a disinheritance form signed by "Julian Oakmoore" sits on the dining table (ch07:217–219); the divorce papers with Marelyn's signature that he half-expected are not there (ch07:221–223). Marelyn's study is unlocked, her purse wall bare, the scuff marks touched up with new paint (ch07:225); in the bedroom his things are neatly folded and stacked and hers are all gone (ch07:227). He jokes that she is probably just filing for divorce at court (ch07:229); the moment he says it, he collapses and weeps (ch07:231–233). "No job, no family, no support." (ch07:235). His stomach prompts him to cook (ch07:241–247).
8. **Marelyn returns** (ch07:249–335). While he cooks a stir-fry, risotto, and fried potatoes, warm arms wrap his waist: "Why does eviction smell so sublime?" (ch07:251–253). He fears divorce papers (ch07:255). Marelyn is cheerful, calls it a "lovely deal" for her purses, and says "the look on father's face was priceless when we filed the disinheritance form at court." (ch07:277–283). Carden sees a second disinheritance form, from the Winston family, signed by Charles Winston (ch07:289). Marelyn says Charles refused to let the family name get tarnished and told her to join Carden "in those circumstances" if she insisted on staying with him (ch07:291). Carden asks if she doesn't want a divorce; she scowls "Why in the Father's name would I divorce you?" (ch07:295–297). Marelyn says she was too devastated; that she prayed all night he would keep his job so she'd have an excuse not to do the right thing; that "You made the right choice. We made the right choice." (ch07:305–317). She says "I was never going to divorce you. Especially not after I told you to approve Annie and Roger's latest case. I promised." (ch07:323–325). They clasp hands and Carden says, "Thank you." (ch07:331–335).

**Chapter outcome.** Carden is terminated, disinherited by Julian, and Marelyn is disinherited by the Winstons; the marriage holds.

### Chapter Eight – When Faith Is Rewarded

*Place:* Windbury (café, streets, a hill outside the city, Horace's mayoral office). *Time:* a day in late autumn (ch08:72), roughly a week after Horace's warning (ch08:44).

1. **The café** (ch08:4–60). In a café Carden and Marelyn face six of their friends and classmates, who press him as Annie tells him not to joke (ch08:4–10). Marelyn says they are disinherited, homeless, and unemployed (ch08:12). Roger offers the old flat he and Annie lived in as rental accommodation (ch08:16–20). Carden asks if they have money; Marelyn says yes, she sorted everything out (ch08:22–28). The group's cash flow: arrears to the Customs and Revenue Board were paid but funds for the year's charity are severely short (ch08:36); Jericho says they cannot cover winter (ch08:38); Mary adds it is when people are most vulnerable (ch08:40). Marelyn asks if they discussed it with Mayor Horace (ch08:42). Carden says Horace is ultimately responsible (ch08:46). Annie asks "Like how he dealt with last year's riots?" (ch08:50). Carden says Horace cares more than they realise and tells Roger in Arcane Tongue that Horace was ready to support their welfare program in Government had he known; Roger translates (ch08:54–56). Newton says they erred by excluding Horace and asks Carden to advocate for them (ch08:56). Roger says they will defer to Carden and Marelyn's judgment (ch08:60).
2. **The walk and the hill** (ch08:62–98). Annie and Roger promise to tidy the old flat by evening (ch08:62). Carden and Marelyn brainstorm with no result (ch08:62). They walk hand in hand through Windbury, seeing shuttered businesses, charred husks, soup-kitchen queues and unrepaired craters (ch08:68). They climb a hill outside the city and see Windbury below (ch08:72). Carden says Annie and he came here as teenagers; he calls it "Now it's ours." (ch08:78–80). Marelyn says that while he and his old flame gallivanted there, she nagged a young baker at his family's downtown bakery for free croissants, hazelnut-cream ones being the most expensive (ch08:84). She asks what it said about her that she was instantly smitten by someone who indulged her without asking anything (ch08:86). Carden says it is probably what people said about him when he promised to marry the first person who joined his daydreams (ch08:88). Marelyn says "Let's make this work." and Carden repeats it (ch08:94–98).
3. **The proposal to Horace** (ch08:100–168). In the carriage Marelyn calls it a gamble because Horace is a stickler for rules (ch08:102). In Horace's disordered mayoral office, Horace says he tried to show Edward that Carden's work performed miracles for Windbury (ch08:108–110). Horace says it is a shame he and Carden must endure these times after the mainland (ch08:112). He is touched Marelyn still has his purse; she says it goes with her wardrobe (ch08:114–116). Carden proposes collaboration between City Hall and Bakers For You, Jericho's Clothiers, Mary's Pharmacy and three others: clothing, food, medicine, shoes, and other services redirected for welfare, partly subsidized by the municipal budget to fill the gap once covered by tax exemptions (ch08:124–126). Welfare recipients must volunteer as public servants to restore unrepaired property damage from the riots (ch08:126). The narration says Carden and Marelyn came up with that condition and that "Perhaps Annie, Roger, and the rest believed charity needed to be unconditional." (ch08:128). Horace objects that not everyone complicit in the riots was brought to justice (ch08:130). Carden argues they are desperate people, not pathological convicts, and invokes the mainland shift from punitive to rehabilitative justice (ch08:132–136). Horace asks who will supervise; Carden and Marelyn offer themselves as unemployed people who know money and government (ch08:140–148). Horace laughs: "Carden, you scallywag!" (ch08:152–154). He asks why Marelyn would stoop to such work; she says she has walked away, disowned, and asks for his help (ch08:156–160). Horace fetches a bulging folder and says "Consider your proposal approved. I'll notify your friends personally. You both start tomorrow." (ch08:162–164). "Welcome to the trenches. Have fun." (ch08:168).

**Chapter outcome.** The coalition's welfare program is approved with Carden and Marelyn as supervisors; the couple resolves to make the marriage work.

### Epilogue – Magic Is Self-Actualization

*Place:* Windbury — Chartwell Street construction site, then Carden and Marelyn's rented flat. *Time:* winter, three months into the reconstruction (ep:12); the welfare-for-volunteering program launched "autumn last year" (ep:16).

1. **The marketplace roof** (ep:4–12). Carden sits on a recently installed bench after physical exertion; a cranking, biofuel-engine-powered crane hoists a fifteen-ton steel lattice onto brick arcades (ep:6). Marelyn pauses her heated argument with a municipal contractor to watch; Carden winks at her, she rolls her eyes and sneaks a smile (ep:8). Orange-haired Elf slaves crawl onto the lattice and with Empowerment Magic gracefully catch tempered-glass panes tossed up by their compatriots; sky-blue-haired Elf slaves seal the panes with conventional tools and Enchanting Magic (ep:10). Horace shamed affluent families into loaning Elf slaves (ep:10). Three months of work and the place starts to look like the open-air public marketplace Horace commissioned; floor-tiles, electrical wiring, and a paintjob still remain before Chartwell Street's reconstruction is complete in spring (ep:12).
2. **Renee** (ep:14–26). Carden thanks everyone and infuses his voice with Zeal Magic (ep:14–16). The welfare-for-volunteering program launched "autumn last year" (ep:16). He stops at a half-Elf young woman with boyishly short auburn hair, wearing military fatigues (ep:18). Horace convinced the Black Hounds to train its Arcanist recruits in Windbury's reconstruction work; Zeal Magic and Law Magic keep groups of unfamiliar volunteers focused, motivated, and disciplined (ep:18). He tells her "You did well running this crew, Private Renee." and advises her to run Zeal Magic at a lower pace so she can sustain it longer (ep:20). She salutes and thanks him, in Arcane Tongue, for stepping in when she flagged (ep:22). Carden reflects how far she has come from instigating riots at soup-kitchens (ep:24). He says "You'll be running these without my supervision in no time. Keep up the good work, Renee Rose." (ep:26).
3. **The domestic scene** (ep:28–38). Carden and Marelyn walk home through the cold dusk; the chill smothers Marelyn's temper from the contractor confrontation (ep:28). Carden cooks mutton curry; Marelyn says "This is sublime" and practically inhales it (ep:30). Carden stares at the flat's peeling wallpaper; Marelyn tells him "Stop it… Enjoy what you made instead"; Carden laughs at himself (ep:30–34). After showering, Carden massages Marelyn's knotted shoulders while she reads closing stock-prices (ep:36). They fall asleep together in the flat — Carden notes he sleeps better in this compressed living-space, and that Annie and Roger moved somewhere bigger when they had their first child almost two decades ago (ep:38).

**Chapter outcome.** The Epilogue dramatizes Windbury's reconstruction under Carden and Marelyn's joint supervision and closes with a domestic scene showing the couple's daily life in reduced but contented circumstances. The riot instigator now serves as a Black Hounds private. The broader war is not mentioned.

## Part 5 — State of the story at the end of the manuscript

- **Resolved by the end of the Epilogue:** the first and second tax-exemption petitions (approved, then annulled by Edward); Carden's civil-service career (terminated, Ch7); the disinheritances (Julian's and Charles's); the question of whether Marelyn will leave Carden (she will not, Ch7); housing and income (a rental flat from Roger and Annie, ch08:20, and employment under Horace, ch08:164); Renee's fate (a Black Hounds private, ep:18); the marriage as lived daily reality (the Epilogue's domestic closing scene, ep:28–38).
- **Left open:** whether the world war the *Scriptures* foretell ever breaks out (the last explicit reference is Edward's remark in ch07:34, and the Epilogue is silent); the identity of "Alfred" (ch07:106, 143) is never confirmed; whom Orwell was and who killed him is never stated beyond Annie's cry (ch03:30); the relationship between Carden and Julian and Jane after the disinheritance, and between Marelyn and her parents beyond the disinheritance, is not shown again; the outcome of Edward's rearmament and tax policy; what became of the Imperial *Scripture*'s demand to dissolve Alfainia's government.

## Part 6 — Textual anomalies that affect how the plot is read

These are discrepancies *inside* the manuscript itself, noted so plot summaries are read correctly:

1. **Speaker attribution, Chapter Six.** ~~ch06:190 originally read "Edward blinked at the water" but the speaker in context (ch06:182–196) is Horace.~~ **Corrected in the revised manuscript**: ch06:190 now reads "Horace blinked at the water and then said, 'Apologies, I suppose.'"
2. **Number of exemptions.** Chapter Six's petition covers the Bakers' enterprise plus five other friends' enterprises (ch06:12, 42), yet the forms that return with Edward's approval are counted as "the five exemption forms" (ch06:130); Chapter Seven refers to "the six tax-exemptions" (ch07:52).
3. **Termination vs. resignation.** Chapter Seven depicts a termination letter that Edward signs (ch07:52, 157, 183–185), but in Chapter Eight Marelyn mentions Carden's "resignation" (ch08:24), and Carden elsewhere in Chapter One and Chapter Six had thought of resigning (ch01:96, ch06:10).
4. **Liberation chronology.** The liberation is "seventy-four-years ago" (ch01:52); Charles says "fifty, sixty years ago" (ch04:168); the narrator mentions "the past fifty-four years" (ch04:170); Saint Alfred vanished "more than five-decades ago" (ch07:133); Saint Alfred freed the nation "more than a hundred years ago" from the old Imperium (ch04:168); the occupation lasted nine years (ch05:128).

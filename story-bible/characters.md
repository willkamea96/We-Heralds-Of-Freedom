# Characters

Reference notes compiled from a full sequential read of Draft Zero. Chapter (Ch) references point
to first/major appearances, not every mention.

## POV character

### Carden Oakmoore
- Special Commissioner of Income Tax, Customs and Revenue Board, Portsmouth (Ch1); formerly a
  soldier in the 309th Infantry Regiment, 8th Infantry Division — "the Black Hounds" — specializing
  in pacifying occupied enemy settlements on the mainland (Ch1).
- Arcanist; Calling is an "Ideals Arcanist" who wields Zeal Magic (sky-blue aura), used both to
  intimidate (interrogation of Laplace, Ch2) and to encourage/bolster (Epilogue, coaching Renee).
  Confirmed an Arcanist by Edward Cook ("The Black Hounds. So you're an Arcanist from that regiment,"
  Ch1); Carden's own Arcanist sense detects Edward's presence "stirring the air's Aether" (Ch1).
- From Windbury (Ostnir province); estranged from his wealthy parents Julian and Jane Oakmoore, who
  are secret worshippers of the Creator (Ch4).
- Arranged-married to Marelyn Winston for 25 years at the novella's start; the marriage was loveless
  at first but deepens across the book into genuine partnership (Ch1–8).
- Former teenage sweetheart of Annie (now Annie Baker); the two had promised to marry before Carden
  left for the army (referenced Ch6).
- Wears a plain gold wedding ring (discarded Ch4) later replaced with a custom entwined-snake
  gold/silver band he designs with Marelyn (Ch4).
- Resigns/is terminated from the civil service (Ch7) after signing tax exemptions for the Baker-led
  Windbury charity coalition against Edward Cook's orders; disinherited by Julian in the same
  stretch (Ch7).
- Rebuilds with Marelyn in Windbury: the two broker a public–private welfare/reconstruction scheme
  with Mayor Horace Livingstone and run it themselves (Ch8); by the Epilogue he personally
  supervises reconstruction labor crews (building Windbury's new glass-roofed marketplace) using
  Zeal Magic to sustain workers.
- Devout in the Father's faith underneath his bureaucratic cynicism; his decisive turn comes at
  Saint Alfred's Cathedral in Northern Portsmouth (Ch7), where he receives a blessing from a bearded
  stranger who gives his name only as "Alfred" and reads a Scripture bequeathed by "Alfred Essex" as
  "the humble messenger" of the Father —
  strongly implied to be Saint Alfred himself, alive and unrecognized (Ch7).

## Non-POV principal supporting character

### Marelyn Winston (Oakmoore)
- Carden's wife of 25 years, arranged by their families; from an "old nobility" background, daughter
  of Charles and Beatrice Winston. Never a POV character — experienced entirely through Carden's
  close third-person perspective throughout the manuscript.
- Manages the couple's investment portfolio personally and skillfully; described as far sharper with
  money "than any three" of Carden's older brothers (Ch4). Visits the Stock Exchange on Excelsior
  Avenue daily.
- Politically passionate from Ch1: on the Parliament balcony she challenges Carden — "You served
  several combat-tours on the mainland... So why didn't you say anything back there, Carden?" and
  "So that's it, end of story? The distinguished veteran... shrugs off a betrayal of founding
  principles on the argument he is following orders?" — establishing moral conviction that runs
  through her arc.
- Collects luxury purses (motif) — accumulated over the marriage and displayed on a full wall in her
  private study (the second bedroom, claimed early in the marriage as her own space, Ch5); Horace
  Livingstone buys her purses as a "gentleman's duty" while Carden is at work (the indigo purse,
  Ch2), contributing recent additions to the collection.
- Privately resents her family's and in-laws' hypocrisy and the performative piety/politics around
  her; early in the book conceals strong opinions behind a deliberately "inane" public persona
  (Ch1–2).
- Learns in Portsmouth that Roger Baker — whom she knew as a teenager (revealed Ch8) — is now
  happily married to Carden's old flame Annie (Ch2); her emotional breakdown is triggered not by
  Annie's existence but by learning Roger is married, revealing her own suppressed feelings and
  long-buried unhappiness ("Why can't I be me?"). The same unresolved grief is dramatized again
  when Carden and Marelyn visit Bakers For You in Windbury (Ch4): seeing Roger with Annie and
  their four teenage children leaves Marelyn uncharacteristically lost for words, and she refuses
  to go inside despite Roger's offer of hazelnut-cream croissants — the same pastries she used to
  seek out from him as a teenager (Ch8). On the train home she tells Carden she is "ready to be
  happy."
- Later learns of the Oakmoore family's secret Creator-worship on the same Windbury visit (Ch4).
  Initiates reconciliation with Carden after the Windbury revelations (Ch4: they discard their old
  wedding rings and have new ones made together).
- Gathers intelligence independently during the Ch5 crisis: visits her father Charles at Parliament,
  brother Jeremy at the Admiralty, officers' wives, and her social circle, returning with a
  comprehensive geopolitical picture Carden could not have assembled alone ("Everyone's gone mad,"
  Ch5).
- Reveals on the Ch8 hilltop that she knew Roger Baker as a teenager — she used to visit his family
  bakery for free hazelnut-cream croissants — and was "instantly smitten by someone who indulged me
  without asking for anything in return," a structural parallel to Carden's teenage attachment to
  Annie.
- Disowned by her father Charles after Carden's termination; rather than divorcing Carden as he
  fears, she sells her entire purse collection, files her own disinheritance from the Winstons, and
  recommits to the marriage (Ch7: "I was never going to divorce you").
- Becomes Carden's full partner in the Windbury reconstruction/welfare scheme, co-designing its
  "volunteer labor for aid" structure and personally pitching it to Horace (Ch8). Does not appear
  in the Epilogue.

## Family — Oakmoore

### Julian Oakmoore
- Carden's father; wealthy founder of Oakmoore Industries (military-contract supplier); an Arcanist
  a full Tier above Carden, Calling tied to Armaments Magic (manifests dusk-orange full plate armor
  and a broadsword, Ch5).
- Secret worshipper of the Creator; keeps a hidden shrine (a golden eighteen-branched tree icon)
  behind his study bookcase, concealed via a mechanically activated bookcase (tapped in a particular
  sequence, Ch4).
- Was an Imperial colonist who chose to stay in Alfainia after liberation; built his fortune from
  nothing after Imperium Divinus abandoned its colonists (acknowledged obliquely by Carden, Ch5).
- Spent 25 years quietly converting the Winston family to the Creator's faith (revealed Ch4).
- Forced the arranged marriage between Carden and Marelyn (stated directly, Ch1, Ch5).
- Confronts Carden in the Shadow World over his own hypocrisy (Ch5); draws a Magic broadsword on
  Carden and cuts his neck before Carden disarms him with Zeal Magic.
- Formally disinherits Carden (the disinheritance form is the instrument of the Ch7 rupture, Ch7).
  A second set of papers found alongside Julian's is Charles Winston's disinheritance of Marelyn.

### Jane Oakmoore
- Carden's mother; openly disdainful of Marelyn's financial career and childlessness (Ch4).

## Family — Winston

### Charles Winston
- Marelyn's father; old nobility, newly elected to Parliament for the Justice party (Ch4); secretly
  converted to the Creator's faith by Julian and Jane (Ch4).
- Openly bitter about Alfainia's "decline" and nostalgic for the old Imperium (Ch4); disowns Marelyn
  after Carden's termination; Marelyn is summoned in person to Julian's penthouse for lunch where
  the families deliver the ultimatum (Ch7).

### Beatrice Winston
- Marelyn's mother; echoes Charles's and Jane's criticism of Marelyn's "unwomanly" career and
  behavior (Ch4).

### Jeremy Winston
- Marelyn's brother; promoted to Rear Admiral in the same year as the novella's events, per Julian
  (Ch4). Marelyn visits him at the Admiralty for information (Ch5, off-page).

### Eleanor Winston and Gladwell
- Marelyn's sister and brother-in-law; run a pharmaceutical company that wins lucrative long-term
  military contracts during the rearmament buildup (mentioned Ch4).

## Windbury circle

### Annie Baker (née unspecified)
- Carden's teenage sweetheart; now married to Roger Baker. Co-runs Bakers For You, a Windbury pastry
  factory that diverts 5% of production to the city's poor and runs a soup kitchen (Ch2).
- Has four teenage children with Roger (unnamed individually).
- Seeks Carden out twice for tax relief: first for Bakers For You alone (Ch2, granted Ch4), then for
  a six-enterprise citywide welfare coalition (Ch6, approved by Carden against Edward's wishes,
  triggering Carden's downfall in Ch7).
- Present at Windbury Cathedral during the riot/air-raid (Ch3), where her family friend "Orwell" is
  reported killed.

### Roger Baker
- Annie's husband; a fellow Arcanist (confirmed via military tattoo: the Fifth Marine Regiment, "the
  Blue Kingfishers," Ch2) who can therefore be told classified information in Arcane Tongue.
- More measured than Annie in negotiating with Carden; accompanies the coalition in Ch8.

### Horace Livingstone
- Carden's old army friend (Black Hounds); a fellow veteran and Arcanist, formerly also a Special
  Commissioner of Income Tax before winning election as Mayor of Windbury (Ch2) — an election Carden
  learns was effectively rigged by Parliament's campaign funding and rivals' "legal issues" (Ch3).
- Thrice divorced; dotes on Marelyn with shopping trips and gifts (the indigo purse, Ch2) while
  professing old-fashioned views about women's roles (Ch2).
- Commands martial-law forces in Windbury during the Deliverance Day riots (Ch3); personally
  interrogates the half-Elf ringleader and her parents using Law Magic ("Confess"). Her Human father
  and enslaved Elf mother are forced to falsely confess and then released; the ringleader herself is
  taken away — not executed (no gunfire accompanies her removal) but rather conscripted (Ch3).
- Warns Carden privately that Edward is furious over the tax exemptions (Ch6), setting up Carden's
  ultimatum.
- Approves Carden and Marelyn's welfare-and-reconstruction proposal and hires them to run it (Ch8).

### Renee Rose
- Introduced unnamed as the half-Elf instigator of the Windbury riot (Ch2: auburn hair, almond eyes,
  a fellow Ideals Arcanist using Zeal Magic on the crowd); captured and presumably conscripted by
  Horace's forces rather than executed (Ch3, implied by Carden's remark to Roger: "the Black
  Hounds... won't let such a talented Ideals Arcanist go to waste").
- Reappears by name in the Epilogue as "Private Renee Rose," now bearing the Black Hounds tattoo,
  leading a reconstruction labor crew under Carden's supervision — closing her arc from rioter to
  soldier/public servant.

### Minor Windbury businesspeople (Ch8 welfare coalition)
- **Jericho** — owns a textile factory ("Jericho's Clothiers").
- **Mary** — runs a pharmaceutical distribution company with her husband.
- **Newton** — lanky, richly-haired; runs a shoe factory.
- (Three further unnamed enterprises are referenced as part of the six-business coalition.)

### Mister Laplace
- A furniture-firm owner who petitions Carden for a tax exemption (Ch2) and is exposed by Carden for
  falsifying financial records to hide a shrinking, not growing, business; arrested on the spot.

## Government and clergy

### Edward Cook
- New Finance Minister and head of the Trade Party after the election in Ch1; an Arcanist (his
  presence "stirs Aether," Ch1).
- Delivers the God of War's Scripture commanding war preparations (Ch1) and later the Creator's
  Scripture that mysteriously appears in Parliament (Ch5).
- Drives the tax-hike policy throughout; ultimately gives Carden a binary choice — annul the Baker
  coalition's exemptions and collect arrears, or be terminated — while making clear the exemptions
  will be annulled regardless (Ch7). Accepts Carden's termination with apparent genuine respect ("I
  thank you for your service," spoken in Arcane Tongue, Ch7).

### Terence Howell
- Newly-elected Prime Minister (Labor Party) invested in Ch1's opening ceremony, which the God of War
  personally interrupts and blesses — an unprecedented deviation from ritual.

### Feroz Surena
- Dwarf Lord Magi over Alfainia, the God of War's representative who conducts the investiture
  ritual (Ch1).

### "Alfred" (implied Saint Alfred Essex)
- A middle-aged, bearded stranger in rough workman's overalls who blesses Carden at Saint Alfred's Cathedral
  (Ch7), gives his name simply as "Alfred," and is followed immediately by Carden's discovery that
  the cathedral's Scripture was personally bequeathed by "Alfred Essex," "the humble messenger" of
  the Father, upon the cathedral's founding — and that Saint Alfred vanished mysteriously over fifty
  years ago. The statue outside is strongly implied to resemble the man Carden just met. Left
  ambiguous/unconfirmed in-text; flagged here as a major open thread.

## Background figures referenced but not appearing on-page
- Chef Wheatfield — owner of Wheatfield's Bistro, Carden's favorite restaurant; gives Carden a free
  farewell meal after his termination (Ch7).
- Commander Shapur Mihran — Sarhese Eighteenth Aerial Fleet commander who shadows the Imperial
  flotilla off Windbury (Ch3).

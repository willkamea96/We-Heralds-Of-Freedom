# Timeline

Chronological reconstruction from a full sequential read of Draft Zero. The text marks time mostly
by season and by explicit "X months/days later" phrases rather than calendar dates; seasons below are
inferred from those markers, starting arbitrarily at "Year 0" for the investiture ritual.

## Deep background (before the story opens)
- **~100+ years ago** (per Charles Winston, loosely) — Saint Alfred leads Alfainia's founding/liberation
  from "the old Imperium's rot"; the nation is named after him.
- **~54 years before Ch1** — The historic compact between Mankind ("Heralds of Freedom") and
  Dwarfkind ("Pillars of Iron") is struck, backed by the God of War, marking Alfainia's liberation
  from Imperium Divinus's nine-year occupation. Julian and Jane Oakmoore, Imperial colonists, choose
  to stay and are left with nothing when Imperium Divinus withdraws; they rebuild as model citizens
  and found Oakmoore Industries.
- **~54 years before Ch1** (same period as the liberation) — Saint Alfred vanishes "during Alfainia's
  liberation" (Ch7), reportedly embittered that Alfainia secured its freedom via the God of War
  rather than the Father. Ch1's precise "fifty-four-years ago" aligns with Ch4's "the past
  fifty-four years" (ch04:170) and is consistent with Ch7's "more than five-decades ago" and
  Charles Winston's loose "fifty, sixty years ago" (Ch4).
- **Teenage years, then the army (before the arranged marriage)** — As teenagers in Windbury
  Cathedral, Carden and Annie vow to marry once Carden returns from the army (Ch6). Carden then
  serves multiple combat tours on the mainland ("before we married," Ch1) with the 309th Infantry
  Regiment/8th Infantry Division ("the Black Hounds"), alongside Horace Livingstone and Roger Baker
  (different units: Horace also Black Hounds; Roger, the Fifth Marine Regiment, "Blue Kingfishers").
  The vow goes unkept — Carden's "broken promise" (Ch2) — and the arranged marriage to Marelyn takes
  its place. Annie later marries Roger.
- **~25 years before Ch1** — Carden and Marelyn's families force their arranged marriage; around the
  same time, Julian and Jane begin quietly converting the Winston family to the Creator's faith (a
  process they describe as gradual, "little by little for twenty-five years," Ch4).
- **~8 years before Ch1** (per an elderly Windbury commuter, Ch2) — The countryside's visible decline
  (unrepaired Commune defenses, fallow fields, crumbling infrastructure) begins, regardless of which
  party holds government.

## Year 0 — Winter: Deliverance Day and the Windbury riots (Chapters 1–3)
- **Investiture ritual, Portsmouth** — Terence Howell (Labor) is invested as Prime Minister; Feroz
  Surena officiates. The God of War's divine presence manifests for the first time in the ritual's
  history,
  staggering Alfainia's resident Demigods and blessing Terence's cabinet. Edward Cook (new Finance
  Minister) summons Carden, already the sitting Special Commissioner of Income Tax, and shows him the
  God of War's
  *Scripture* ordering Alfainia to mobilize for an unnamed world war against the Creator.
- **Following ~three months** — Carden begins enforcing Edward's new corporate tax hikes across Alfainia;
  Horace Livingstone wins (effectively engineered) election as Mayor of Windbury; Annie and Roger
  Baker petition Carden for a tax exemption for Bakers For You's charity work; Carden agrees to visit
  Windbury for Deliverance Day to assess their case in person.
- **The Friday before Deliverance Day, Portsmouth (Ch2)** — After Annie and Roger's visit to his
  office, Carden is questioned obliquely by Marelyn over dinner and mentions that Annie is "happily
  married to one Roger Baker." Marelyn already knows Roger (she gives an accurate physical
  description) and breaks down in tears ("Why can't I be me?"). The text never states the cause;
  the scene's order and Ch4/Ch8 support reading it as tied to Roger's marriage as well as to her own
  long-buried unhappiness. The next morning she declines to come to Windbury ("I'd rather not run
  into our parents. Or Roger and Annie."), laces her fingers in Carden's and tells him, "Safe
  travels. Get home soon."
- **Deliverance Day weekend, Windbury** — Carden witnesses the city's severe economic decline firsthand.
  At the Bakers' soup kitchen, a rock is thrown at Carden (the government official; he ducks and it
  strikes Roger's chest); Carden intervenes with Zeal Magic and confronts a half-Elf ringleader
  (later identified as Renee Rose) who matches his own Magic; explosions and wider rioting erupt
  across the city immediately after,
  revealed to be deliberately timed — Horace's Black Hounds troops are already pre-positioned in
  Windbury "the same day" the riot breaks out, implying foreknowledge/engineering of the unrest.
- **Same evening** — Martial law. Carden first telegraphs Marelyn that he is safe and will take the
  Sunday 4 PM train home, then shelters with Annie's family, old classmates, and Windbury's
  affluent at Windbury Cathedral; a friend of the family, "Orwell," is killed by the Black Hounds.
  Horace captures and interrogates three riot suspects via Law Magic, with Carden also participating:
  Renee Rose's enslaved Elf mother and Human father voluntarily make false confessions to protect her;
  Law Magic exposes their lies (both retch). Carden reassures them — "We will not harm your daughter"
  — and they recant. Renee Rose (an Arcanist) then confesses truthfully and is taken away (implicitly
  rather than executed — see `characters.md`); her parents are released.
- **That night** — An Imperial naval/air flotilla approaches Windbury's coast; Alfainian and Sarhese
  forces scramble to shadow it; the Imperials vanish behind an Atmospheric Magic storm without
  engaging. Marelyn's reply telegram, announcing she is coming on the first available Sunday train,
  reaches the Windbury telegraph office overnight.
- **Next morning (Sunday)** — Carden finds Marelyn's telegram waiting ("Please wait for me"); she
  arrives in Windbury on the first available Sunday train (Ch4).

## Year 0 — Late winter: Homecoming (Chapter 4)
- Carden briefs Marelyn on the riot and on his parents' secret Creator-worship before they visit the
  Oakmoore estate. Julian reveals he and Jane have spent 25 years converting the Winston family to
  the Creator's faith; Marelyn confronts him over exploiting her family's hardship. The couple then
  visits the Winstons, who praise the Imperium and belittle Marelyn's career and her "failing as a
  wife"; Marelyn, trembling, manages only a few flat lines before dragging Carden out and slamming
  the door.
- Walking back, Carden and Marelyn each discard their old wedding rings (gifts from their parents)
  and commission matching new entwined gold-and-silver bands from a Windbury jeweler classmate.
  Carden approves Annie and Roger's original tax-exemption case. The couple visits Bakers For You,
  where Marelyn sees Roger with Annie and their four teenage children; she is uncharacteristically
  lost for words and refuses to go inside despite Roger's offer of hazelnut-cream croissants, which
  he remembers she loved. (Ch8 later has her recall free hazelnut-cream croissants from "a certain
  young baker" at the family's downtown bakery — implicitly Roger, though the text never names him
  or gives her age.) On the train home, Marelyn tells Carden she's "ready to be happy."

## Year 0 — Spring: Escalation (Chapter 5)
- Portsmouth suffers its first Imperial air-fleet incursion — airships of various displacements
  skirt the harbor at extreme distance (previously confined to provincial coasts like Windbury).
  A mysterious *Scripture* from the Creator appears
  unexplained in Parliament, demanding Alfainia's submission or "Sacred War." Edward tells Carden
  Alfainia must accelerate rearmament to avoid destabilizing its Dominions, funded by further tax
  hikes (bonds being politically unworkable).
- Julian uses a Shadow Magic ring artifact to pull Carden into the Shadow World from his office;
  Julian gloats about the coming societal collapse, conjures an Armaments Magic broadsword, and
  presses it to Carden's neck (the wound cuts deep). Carden rebukes him in Arcane Tongue; his Zeal
  Magic erodes Julian's confidence, and Carden smacks the trembling blade's hilt from Julian's
  hands. Carden is treated by an enslaved Elf healer.
- That evening, Marelyn — having spent the day gathering intelligence from her father, brother, and
  social circle — tells Carden "everyone's gone mad" and that she feels "exactly where I need to be."
  The two exchange a private vow to face the future together.
- **~3 months later, start of summer** — The first Dominion reports an Imperial airship incursion;
  more follow in quick succession until the whole Commonwealth is understood to be under undeclared
  siege, triggering further mobilization. Annie and Roger return to Carden's office (opens Chapter 6).

## Year 0 — Summer to early autumn: The second case (Chapter 6)
- Annie and Roger, now representing a six-enterprise coalition (including Jericho's textile factory
  and Mary's pharmaceutical distribution company), ask Carden for exemptions for a citywide Windbury
  welfare program. Carden is reluctant given scrutiny over military spending; Marelyn, after
  reviewing the coalition's books (she finds the numbers "perfectly legitimate") and vouching for its
  third-party auditor, tells him to "just do the right thing." Carden signs; Edward unexpectedly
  countersigns approval without comment.
- Annie, Roger, Carden, and Marelyn give thanks together at Saint Alfred's Cathedral in Portsmouth;
  Carden acknowledges his faith in the Father has weakened ("perhaps not as wholehearted as what the
  Father would like"), though he denies having abandoned the Father for the God of War entirely.
- **Fall** — Alfainia holds a lavish military "maiden voyage" parade in Portsmouth Harbor showcasing
  the year's rearmament; public reception is muted/weary rather than enthusiastic.
- **Mid-autumn** — Horace visits Carden in Portsmouth to warn him that Edward has audited Windbury's
  finances and is furious about the tax exemptions; Edward summons Carden immediately after.

## Year 0 — Autumn: The reckoning (Chapter 7)
- Edward gives Carden an ultimatum: annul all the Baker-coalition exemptions and personally collect
  the arrears, or be terminated — making clear the exemptions will be annulled either way. Carden
  takes one
  night to decide.
- That afternoon, Carden walks home early and finds Marelyn already there; she reveals both fathers
  sought her out and brought her to Julian's Portsmouth penthouse for lunch; they present her with
  an unsigned disinheritance form (Julian's, for Carden) and an unsigned divorce form, along with the
  message: "There is no room for a disgraced prodigal son." She asks for the night alone.
- Carden wanders to Saint Alfred's Cathedral in Northern Portsmouth, is blessed by a stranger calling
  himself "Alfred," and learns the cathedral's founding Scripture was personally left by "Alfred
  Essex" — implicitly the vanished Saint Alfred himself.
- **Next morning** — Carden is terminated rather than annul the exemptions; Edward accepts the
  termination with apparent respect, in Arcane Tongue. Carden is given two days to vacate his
  civil-service flat; he returns home to find Julian's disinheritance already executed, Marelyn's
  belongings removed — and notably, no divorce papers (their absence signals Marelyn is not leaving).
- **That evening** — Marelyn returns: she had in fact spent the day selling her purses (though the
  indigo purse Horace gave her stays with her) and filing her father Charles's signed disinheritance
  of her at court — Charles refused to let the family name be "tarnished" and signed the form
  himself; Marelyn embraced the outcome and
  defiantly filed it ("the look on father's face was priceless," Ch7) — she was never filing for
  divorce. The two recommit to each other.

## Year 0 — Late autumn: Rebuilding in Windbury (Chapter 8)
- Carden and Marelyn meet the Baker-led coalition (Roger, Jericho, Mary, Newton, and others) in
  Windbury; the coalition, now also strained for funds heading into winter (peak welfare demand),
  realizes it never looped in Mayor Horace Livingstone and asks Carden and Marelyn to broker a deal
  with him.
- At Carden and Annie's old teenage retreat on a hill outside Windbury (now claimed as his and
  Marelyn's own: "Now it's ours"), Carden and Marelyn reaffirm their
  marriage ("Let's make this work") before pitching Horace a public–private welfare/reconstruction
  scheme: aid conditional on recipients volunteering as public-works labor, co-run and personally
  supervised by Carden and Marelyn (now unemployed and needing income themselves). Horace approves
  and hires them on the spot.
- After signing their employment contracts with Horace, Carden and Marelyn take a rented carriage to
  an older block of flats downtown. Annie and Roger show them the fifth-story flat (Roger and Annie's
  first home, "twenty-some years ago"); the four Baker teenagers shuttle Carden and Marelyn's trunks
  in. The flat compresses bedroom, living-room, dining-room, kitchen, and pantry into one common
  living space — less than half the size of Carden's old civil-service flat — with peeling wallpaper
  but no mold, replaced furniture, showers and piped hot water. Annie: "We replaced all the
  furniture just for you both." Roger: "Just don't forget rent." Standing together at the center of
  the flat, Carden and Marelyn say in unison, without meaning to: "We'll make this work" (Ch8).

## Year 0 into Year 1 — Winter: Epilogue ("Magic Is Self-Actualization")
- Three months into the reconstruction program, Carden personally supervises a labor crew (including
  enslaved Elves with Empowerment/Enchanting Magic) at the construction site for Chartwell Street's
  new glass-roofed public marketplace. A cranking, biofuel-engine-powered crane hoists a fifteen-ton
  steel lattice onto brick arcades; orange-haired Elf slaves catch tempered-glass panes with
  Empowerment Magic; sky-blue-haired Elf slaves seal them onto the lattice with conventional tools
  and Enchanting Magic. Horace shamed affluent families into loaning Elf slaves, and also convinced
  the Black Hounds to train its Arcanist recruits in Windbury's reconstruction work. Marelyn is
  present at the site, arguing with a municipal contractor over inflated invoices.
- Carden coaches Private Renee Rose — the riot's former ringleader, now a Black Hounds soldier
  wearing military fatigues and serving as crew leader — on pacing her own Zeal Magic for sustained
  labor support, closing her arc from insurgent to fellow Ideals Arcanist serving the same rebuilding
  effort Carden now leads.
- That evening, Carden and Marelyn walk home through the cold dusk; Carden cooks mutton curry
  ("This is sublime"); they commiserate over their small rented flat's peeling wallpaper; Marelyn
  tells him to enjoy what he made instead of staring at the walls. After both shower, Carden
  massages Marelyn's knotted shoulders while she reads closing stock-prices. Marelyn asks how close
  he is to self-actualizing his Calling; Carden cleaves his mind and hears the Arcane's whisper:
  "*Stir the masses into action. Unite them behind a better future.*" His spirit trembles as if a
  breakthrough lingers just a hairsbreadth away, yet he has felt this way for weeks — very close
  but missing one piece he cannot identify. Marelyn clasps his hand: "I have no doubt you'll crack
  it. You always do." They fall asleep together in the flat.
- Next morning, Carden makes bruschetta for breakfast. They stop by the newsstands around Windbury
  Square and drink in the headlines: "WORLD AT WAR!" — the Prajnese continent ablaze from a Dharmee
  Raj civil war, Zaltaclan foiling Imperial offensives in the Ihtoacan continent, Imperial forces in
  retreat across the Tianxianese continent, Alfainia and the Commonwealth supporting all wars against
  tyranny. The Creator and His empire at war against three Great Powers simultaneously; Alfainia and
  Sarh never lifted a finger — the Creator did that to Himself. An Alfainian aerial fleet glides
  overhead toward the sea for routine patrol. Carden reflects on the foolishness of mortal planning
  in the face of history's great vagaries.
- Horace tells Carden and Marelyn everything is running better than he dared hope. He praises
  Carden's training of Renee Rose and the other recruits, noting they are practically steering the
  ship. Carden requests raises for himself and Marelyn. Horace laughs but notes his biggest problem
  is getting municipal funding from Edward — he slaps a Parliamentary budgetary file with Charles
  Winston's name on it against his desk. Horace tells them to take the day off while he handles the
  politicking. As they leave, Marelyn elbows Carden for indulging Horace's misogynistic remark about
  convincing women; Carden defends himself by citing the raises he's trying to secure. Marelyn
  retorts that Horace's attitude is why no woman would marry him.
- Carden and Marelyn walk to Windbury Cathedral. The exterior grounds now host orderly queues of
  welfare recipients at cobblers, dentists, doctors, soup kitchens, and tailors — no longer exuding
  the desperate edge of poverty, just dour-faced, hardworking folk in need of some help. Police
  officers patrol helpfully rather than harmfully. Inside, Annie, Roger, Jericho, Mary, and their
  associates run the welfare operation so effectively that Carden and Marelyn are politely shooed
  out — none need their help today.
- In a transept, Carden and Marelyn find Renee, her auburn-haired Human father and green-haired Elf
  mother, and a large group of Elves, half-Elves, and Humans kneeling in prayer before a
  stained-glass window depicting Lucifer's Rebellion — when Lucifer and the ancient Elves rose
  against the Father's world-order. Six months earlier, the supposedly imprisoned Lucifer
  dramatically reappeared in the Zhang Dynasty's capital and pronounced a revelation in Arcane
  Tongue that every Arcanist in Alfainia (and from later reports, all Alfainia) heard despite the
  distance: the Creator was a Human named Gabriel Angelos; the God of War was a Dwarf named Davud
  Abshalom; and Lucifer himself was an Elf named Athanásios toû Zenonos — all extremely powerful
  Arcanists the Father uplifted into divinity as rewards for their service, only for all three to go
  astray. What the Father gave, so could the Father take — as evidenced by Lucifer's own millennia of
  imprisonment, now redeemed by the Father to be His prophet.
- Lucifer pronounced the Father decreed Elf-kind's millennia of penance as slaves over. The Father
  marked Elfkind redeemed. Elfkind was free. Except Alfainia and most of the world never
  acknowledged it — the God of War commanded otherwise and Alfainia's Parliament and upper class
  obeyed. Institutions and prejudice had not yet caught up to divine mandate. Carden's eyes dart
  between the slave-tattoos on every Elf's right wrist.
- The broader war with Imperium Divinus has broken out across multiple continents by the Epilogue's
  close, though its final outcome remains unresolved. Carden's self-actualization of his Zealot
  Calling is described as imminent but incomplete. The Epilogue's focus is the personal/local
  resolution of Carden and Marelyn's marriage, Carden's renewed faith and purpose, and the new
  revelation about Elf slavery's divine origins and its divinely decreed end.

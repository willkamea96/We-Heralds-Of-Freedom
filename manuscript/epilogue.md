# Epilogue – Magic Is Self-Actualization


Carden plopped himself on the recently-installed bench and wiped the sweat from his brow. Despite the winter air's bite on his skin, such sustained physical exertions burned in him something fierce. Thankfully he just needed to help with enough work the work-crew didn't think he was merely barking orders at them all day. Now those same men and women finished the rest without a single word from him.

A cranking, biofuel-engine-powered crane hoisted the giant steel lattice skyward with mighty creaks and groans. Carden's heart slowed to a crawl but that fifteen-ton lattice rested atop the brick arcades with unerring gentleness. Nothing collapsed and he heard no *cracks*, good sign.

Carden caught Marelyn pause her heated argument with a municipal contractor to watch the process. Carden winked at her when she met his gaze. She rolled her eyes and snuck a smile at him before turning back to the subject of her ire. How foolish that greasy-haired potato of a man even tried to sneak funny invoices past Marelyn.

Orange-haired Elf slaves crawled onto the lattice and with their Empowerment Magic gracefully caught large, tempered-glass panes tossed up by their compatriots. Sky-blue-haired Elf slaves with jingling tool-bags then sealed the panes onto the lattice with their conventional tools and generous application of Enchanting Magic. Thank goodness Horace shamed Windbury's affluent families to loan some of their Elf slaves to help with municipal work. Made all this so much faster. Carden thanked the Father said families refused to be outdone in civic merit by welfare-dependent 'commoners'.

At last, three months of slog and now the place finally started to look like the open-air, public marketplace Horace commissioned. Today's finished roof really made all the difference to Carden's eyes. Now they just needed a month for the floor-tiles, three weeks after for electrical wiring, and a week for a proper paintjob. Come spring City Hall could finally celebrate Chartwell Street's completed reconstruction.

"Good job, everyone!" Carden clapped his hands to let everyone know the shift was over. "Record time, too."

Not that it surprised Carden. Ever since they launched the welfare-for-volunteering program autumn last year, so many volunteered that by now most volunteers showed some knack. But extra compliments so people felt appreciated never hurt.

Carden infused Zeal Magic into his voice as he personally thanked each person in today's work-crew. He stopped at one troublesome half-Elf young woman who now kept her auburn-hair trimmed boyishly short and wore military fatigues. Also helped Horace convinced the Black Hounds to train its Arcanist recruits in Windbury's reconstruction work. Nothing like Zeal Magic and Law Magic to keep groups of unfamiliar volunteers focused, motivated, and disciplined.

"You did well running this crew, Private Renee." Carden patted Renee's now-muscled arm and toned his Zeal Magic down for his fellow Ideals Arcanist. "Next time, I recommend running Zeal Magic at a lower pace so you can sustain it longer. A simple push is often enough to buoy skilled people through this level of sustained physical labor."

"Yes, sir," Renee said and saluted with a hand slick with dusty sweat. Renee's expression relaxed a hair and she said in Arcane Tongue, "Thank you for stepping in earlier when you noticed me flagging."

How far this young woman came from her days instigating riots at soup-kitchens.

"My pleasure. You'll be running these without my supervision in no time. Keep up the good work, Renee Rose."

Carden and Marelyn trundled into their flat much chilled by the walk home through the cold dusk. Thankfully the chill smothered Marelyn's temper from putting that contractor in line. How dare the man upcharge glass sealants and weatherproof surface-resins twenty-percent above market-price. Thank goodness these things came to Marelyn like breathing.

"This is sublime," Marelyn said as she practically inhaled the mutton curry Carden cooked for today's dinner. Carden nodded but the flat's peeling wallpaper kept snagging his attention. Insane how after a year renting the place, they still hadn't saved up enough to replace the wallpaper. And this place was less than half the size of his old civil-service flat!

"Stop it." Marelyn scrunched her nose at him. "You staring at the walls won't make them better. Enjoy what you made instead."

Carden buried his face and laughed at himself. Marelyn was too right.

After they both showered—thankfully the place at least came with that, hot piped water, and an in-unit washroom—Carden massaged Marelyn's knotted shoulders in bed while she read the day's closing stock-prices and mulled her moves tomorrow. Praise the Father they still had their investments. Although with how taxes and inflation still refused to drop, Marelyn insisted they needed to keep their money in stocks to stay ahead. Even if it meant suffering the eyesore of peeling wallpaper.

Carden tucked them to bed, Marelyn snuggled into his arms, and he fell asleep like a brick. Funny how he slept better these days in this flat that compressed bedroom, living-room, dining-room, and kitchen into one common living-space. No wonder Annie and Roger moved somewhere bigger when they had their first kid almost two decades ago.

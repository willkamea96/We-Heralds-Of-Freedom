# Epilogue – Magic Is Self-Actualization


Carden plopped himself on the recently-installed wooden bench and wiped the sweat off his brow. Despite the winter air's bite on his skin, such sustained physical exertions burned in him something fierce. Thankfully he just needed to help out enough so the work-crew didn't think he merely barked orders all day. Now those same men and women finished the rest without a single word from him.

A cranking, biofuel-engine-powered crane hoisted the giant steel lattice skyward with mighty creaks and groans. Carden's heart slowed to a crawl but that fifteen-ton lattice rested atop the brick arcades with unerring gentleness. Nothing collapsed and he heard no *cracks*, good sign.

Carden caught Marelyn pause her heated argument with a municipal contractor to watch the process. Carden winked at Marelyn when she turned to him. Marelyn rolled her eyes and snuck a smile across before turning back to the subject of her ire. How foolish that greasy-haired potato in a nice suit even tried to sneak funny invoices past Marelyn.

Orange-haired Elf slaves crawled onto the lattice and with their Empowerment Magic gracefully caught large, tempered-glass panes tossed up by their compatriots. Sky-blue-haired Elf slaves with jingling tool-bags then sealed said lattice onto the walls and those panes onto the lattice with conventional tools and generous doses of Enchanting Magic. Thank goodness Horace shamed Windbury's affluent families to loan some of their Elf slaves for municipal-work. Made all this so much faster. Carden thanked the Father said families refused to be outdone in civic merit by welfare-dependent 'commoners'.

At last, three months of slog and now the place finally started to look like the open-air, public marketplace Horace commissioned. Finishing the roof really made all the difference to Carden's eyes. Now they just needed a month for the floor-tiles, three weeks after for electrical wiring, and a week for the finishing paint. Come spring, City Hall could finally celebrate Chartwell Street's completed reconstruction.

"Good job, everyone!" Carden clapped his hands to let everyone know the shift was over. "Record time, too."

Not that it surprised Carden. Windbury's welfare-for-volunteering initiative already ran a year now. By this point most volunteers who still needed welfare showed some knack for the work. But extra compliments so people felt appreciated never hurt.

Carden infused Zeal Magic into his voice as he personally thanked each person in today's work-crew. Even under their sweat and grime, some beamed back and others jibed Carden with well-meant jokes. Carden stopped at one troublesome half-Elf young woman who now kept her auburn-hair trimmed boyishly short, wore military fatigues, and sported a Black Hounds tattoo on her right wrist.

It also helped tremendously that Horace convinced the Black Hounds to train its Arcanist recruits in Windbury. Nothing like Zeal Magic and Law Magic to keep groups of unfamiliar volunteers focused, motivated, and disciplined.

"You did well running this crew, Private Renee." Carden patted Renee's now-muscled arm and toned his Zeal Magic down for his fellow Ideals Arcanist. "Next time, I recommend running Zeal Magic at a lower pace so you can sustain it longer. A simple push is often enough to buoy skilled people through this level of sustained physical labor."

"Yes, sir," Renee said and saluted with a hand slick with dusty sweat. Renee's expression relaxed and she said in Arcane Tongue, "Thank you for stepping in earlier when you noticed me flagging."

How far this young woman came from her days instigating riots at soup-kitchens.

"My pleasure. You'll be running these without my supervision in no time. Keep up the good work, Renee Rose."

Carden and Marelyn trundled into their flat much chilled by the walk home through frigid dusk. Thankfully the cold smothered Marelyn's temper from wrangling that contractor in line. Carden agreed when Marelyn brought it up for the third time. How dare the man upcharge glass sealants and weatherproof surface-resins twenty-percent above market-value. Carden reminded Marelyn how thankful he was for her. That seemed to put the issue to bed.

"This is sublime," Marelyn said as she practically inhaled the mutton curry Carden cooked for today's dinner. Carden nodded but the flat's peeling wallpaper snagged his attention. Again. Insane how after a year renting the place, they still hadn't saved up enough to replace the wallpaper. Yet this past year he'd already built everything from public housing to bridges!

"Stop it." Marelyn scrunched her nose at him. "You staring at the walls won't make them better."

Marelyn scooped more stew into Carden's plate. "Enjoy what you made instead."

Carden buried his face and laughed at himself. Marelyn was too right.

After they both showered, Carden massaged Marelyn's knotted shoulders in bed while she went through today's closing stock-prices and mulled her moves for tomorrow. Praise the Father they still owned investments to supplement their modest City Hall salaries. Although with how taxes and inflation still refused to drop, Marelyn insisted they needed to squirrel their money into stocks to stay ahead. Even if it meant suffering the eyesore of peeling wallpaper.

"How close are you to self-actualizing your Calling?" Marelyn asked out of the blue. She craned her neck back and her green eyes shimmered at Carden.

Carden closed his eyes and cleansed his mind. He gave space for the Arcane's meek whispers.

"*Stir the masses into action. Unite them behind a better future.*"

Carden's spirit trembled and yearned as if some breakthrough hovered just a hairsbreadth away. Except he'd felt this way for weeks now. He should have been grateful, honestly. Carden already made more progress self-actualizing his *Zealot* Calling this past year in Windbury than the past thirty-something years in the army and civil-service combined. Yet this edging still galled him. What was he missing?

"I'm very close," Carden said when Marelyn's expression turned worried. "I can almost taste it. I'm just missing one little piece, but for the life of me can't figure out what."

Marelyn's warm hand clasped his and she smiled. "I have no doubt you'll crack it."

"You always do."

Carden tucked them to bed, Marelyn snuggled into his arms, and he fell asleep like a brick. Funny how he slept better these days in a place so much smaller than his old one.

Next morning, after Carden made them a breakfast of bruschetta, they threw on their coats and headed straight to City Hall for work. Like every day this past year, they stopped by the newsstands around Windbury Square and drank in the latest headlines together.

"*WORLD AT WAR!*"

"*PRAJNESE CONTINENT ABLAZE FROM DHARMEE RAJ CIVIL WAR.*"

"*ZALTACLAN FOILS YET ANOTHER IMPERIAL OFFENSIVE IN IHTOACAN CONTINENT.*"

"*IMPERIAL FORCES IN RETREAT ACROSS TIANXIANESE CONTINENT.*"

"*ALFAINIA AND THE COMMONWEALTH SUPPORT ALL WARS AGAINST TYRANNY.*"

Carden finished his coffee right as Marelyn downed her chocolate-milk. Almost a year in and newspaper publishers still regurgitated those same headlines. Marelyn sifted past the front-pages for Stock Exchange news while Windbury's morning commuters passed by without a second-glance. Then again, who in their right minds ever imagined reality one day surpassed fiction.

The Creator and His empire at war against three Great Powers simultaneously. Alfainia and Sarh never even lifted a finger. The Creator did that to Himself. Alfainia thus reaped the dividends of continued national peace by lavishly financing the Imperium's foes abroad. Just irked Carden to the bone it meant he and Marelyn continued to suffer these stupidly high taxes.

Horns high above blared as if in reminder. Most of Windbury Square's pedestrians paid them no mind except for some excitable children. Carden looked up regardless and saluted. Marelyn shook her head and scoffed at Carden with a smile. He couldn't help it, old habits died hard.

An Alfainian aerial-fleet glided overhead. Metal hulls emblazoned with Alfainia's national iconography gleamed under the rising sun. Those vessels cruised over Windbury's unimpressed residents towards the sea for their routine patrol. Funny, Alfainia finally possessed more Home-Fleets yet the country and its Dominions no longer suffered Imperial airship incursions.

The foolishness of mortal planning in the face of history's great vagaries.

Horace shook his head when Carden and Marelyn asked for their next assignment. Horace gestured at the neatly-stacked files on his desk and said, "Honestly, everything's running better than I dared hope."

Horace wagged a finger at Carden. "You've done well training Renee Rose and the other recruits. They're practically steering the ship for us at this point."

Carden scratched the back of his head and chuckled. No need to correct Horace that Renee and the others were natural talents. Whoever in the Black Hounds recruited those riot instigators and snapped them into shape deserved all the credit. Horace didn't need to know. Carden still wanted a raise after all.

"I hope you'll remember that when signing raises for myself and Marelyn."

Horace boomed with laughter and even Marelyn visibly fought back a stupid grin. Horace calmed down and slapped a Parliamentary budgetary file with Charles Winston's name on it against his desk. "And therein lies my biggest problem. Asking old Edward for municipal funding is harder than convincing a woman she's wrong."

*I can already imagine how that conversation went and why Edward said no.*

Carden caught that glint in Marelyn's gaze. Guess she concurred with Carden. Carden cleared his throat. "Boggles the mind indeed, Horace old friend. Can't imagine why Edward can't see your good work or why Charles won't advocate for all you've done. Must be the foul air in Portsmouth."

"Indeed." Horace groaned to his feet and ushered Carden and Marelyn out. "Leave the politicking to me. I'll get us more funding one way or another and get you both raises. Take the day off."

As Carden and Marelyn descended City Hall's front-steps, Marelyn elbowed Carden. "Carden, how could you? Indulging Horace's fallacies. Your own friend."

Carden shot Marelyn a wounded look and touched his chest as if hurt. "I'm hurt. Here I am trying to get us both fat raises for next year."

Marelyn scrunched her nose at him. "And that's why your friend will never understand why no woman would ever marry him."

Carden and Marelyn laughed and strolled arm-in-arm to Windbury Cathedral across the Square. Cobblers, dentists, doctors, soup-kitchens, and tailors serviced neat queues of welfare-recipients around the cathedral's exterior grounds. Unlike two years ago, those in line no longer exuded that desperate edge of poverty. Just dour-faced, hardworking folk in need of some help to tide through lean times. Pairs of police-officers strutted around them on patrol, these days more helpful than harmful. Small wins.

Inside the warm cathedral, Carden and Marelyn's friends hustled and bustled like the brains of welfare they were. Annie, Roger, and their teenage-children carted shelf-stable pastries. Jericho and his tailors folded looms of fabrics. Mary and her husband sorted through medicine crates. All that and more. And strangely, just like with Horace, none needed their help today. Just a flurry of thanks that doubled as polite shooing out.

Hence why Carden and Marelyn wandered to a transept where they stumbled upon the oddest collection of individuals. Civilian-clothed Renee, her auburn-haired Human father and green-haired Elf mother, and a large group of Elves, half-Elves, and Humans. All knelt in prayer before a stained-glass window that depicted a rather infamous scene.

Lucifer's Rebellion. When Lucifer and the ancient Elves rose against the Father's world-order however many eons ago.

Carden and Marelyn exchanged a glance. Good, seemed Marelyn agreed the atmosphere deserved some respect. They slunk to the back of this congregation, knelt, and lowered their heads. How could they not? Six months ago, the supposedly imprisoned Lucifer dramatically reappeared in the Zhang Dynasty's capital. As if the world weren't absurd enough, myth became reality.

Only for that rebel to pronounce in Arcane Tongue a revelation that shook the world. Carden shivered from the memory. Carden, Roger, Horace, and every Arcanist in Windbury—and from later reports all Alfainia—heard it, even though Lucifer pronounced his revelation an ocean away.

The Creator was a Human, Gabriel Angelos.

The God of War was a Dwarf, Davud Abshalom.

Lucifer himself was an Elf, Athanásios toû Zenonos.

Extremely powerful Arcanists the Father uplifted into divinity as rewards for their service. Only for all three to go astray. And what the Father gave, so could the Father take. As evidenced by Lucifer's own life, imprisoned for millennia and now redeemed by the Father to be His prophet.

And perhaps the most important revelation for the group in front of Carden.

Lucifer pronounced the Father decreed Elf-kind's millennia of penance as slaves over. The Father marked Elfkind redeemed. Elfkind was free.

Except Alfainia and most of the world never acknowledged it. Carden's eyes darted between the various slave-tattoos inked onto every Elf's right wrist. The God of War commanded otherwise and Alfainia's Parliament and upper class obeyed. Institutions and prejudice hadn't yet caught up to divine mandate.

# Epilogue – Magic Is Self-Actualization


Carden plopped himself on the recently-installed bench and wiped the sweat from his brow. Despite the winter air’s bite on his skin, such sustained physical exertions burned in him something fierce. Thankfully he finished his part, now the work-crew ran the finishing touches. 

Several dozen men and women hoisted with simple cranes the giant steel lattice into the air with mighty creaks and groans. Carden’s heart slowed to a crawl but the crew rested that lattice atop the brick arcades with unerring gentleness. No visible collapses or audible *cracks*, good sign. Orange-haired Elf slaves tossed large tempered-glass panes up to the lattice from ground-level that other orange-haired Elves on top caught without breaking. Those Elves maneuvered the panes onto the lattice whereupon Elf slaves with sky-blue hair melded the panes onto the steel lattice with bare hands, chisels, and files.

Empowerment Magic and Enchanting Magic sure made things so much easier now. Thank goodness Horace shamed Windbury’s affluent families to loan some of their Elf slaves to help with municipal work. Carden thanked the Father said families refused to appear outdone in civic merit by welfare-dependent ‘commoners’.

In the time Carden took to catch his breath, the work-crew finished installing the glass-roof over Windbury’s newest public marketplace.

“Good job, everyone!” Carden clapped his hands to get everyone’s attention. “Record time, too.”

Not that it surprised Carden. With so many of Windbury’s residents involved in volunteer reconstruction over the past year, at this point most who showed up already developed some knack for the work. But extra compliments and making the volunteers feel appreciated never hurt.

Carden infused Zeal Magic into his voice as he personally thanked each individual in today’s work-crew. He stopped at one troublesome half-Elf young woman who now kept her auburn-hair trimmed boyishly short and wore military fatigues. Oh, she finally bore the Black Hounds tattoo on her right-wrist, he missed that earlier at the morning-briefing.

“You did well running this crew, Private Renee.” Carden patted Renee’s now-muscled arm and toned his Zeal Magic down for his fellow Ideals Arcanist. “Next time, I recommend running Zeal Magic at a lower pace so you can sustain it longer. A simple push is often enough to buoy skilled people through this type of sustained physical labor.”

“Yes, sir,” Renee said and saluted with a hand slick with dusty sweat. Renee’s expression relaxed a hair and she said in Arcane Tongue, “Thank you for stepping in earlier when you noticed me flagging.”

How far this young woman came from her days instigating riots at soup-kitchens. 

“My pleasure. Keep the good work, Renee Rose.”

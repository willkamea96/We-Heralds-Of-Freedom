# Chapter Five – Relentless March Of History


Portsmouth suffered its first Imperial air-fleet incursion with spring’s first buds. Like the Windbury air-raid during last winter’s Deliverance Day, Imperial airships of various displacements skirted Portsmouth Harbor at extreme distance. The Alfainian Air-Force and Alfainian First Home-Fleet shadowed it until the Imperial warships vanished behind an Atmospheric Magic-conjured storm.

But that was enough.

Three days later, Carden and Marelyn blinked at the headlines plastered across Excelsior Avenue’s newsstands.

“*BREAKING!*”

“*IMPERIAL AIR-FLEETS SPOTTED OFF ABERCLAIR, GLENFOLD, AND PONTMYR!*”

“*Official imperial readouts **state: ‘**V**essels **were **within **their **rights to inspect the Creator’s** Alfainian provinces**’**.*”

“*Parliament denounces provocations**!** Home-**F**leets mobilized**. Sarh’s Lord Magi **announces**…*”

Carden skimmed through the rest of that article and Marelyn did likewise. They finished and left the newsstand right as the press of nosy bodies got worse.

“This is too comical to be true, it has to be propaganda right, Carden?” Marelyn asked as she finished the last of her buttered croissant.

If it was propaganda, it certainly worked. All around them, Excelsior Avenue’s typically hustling bankers, lawyers, and stock-traders all diverted from their defining brisk paces to flock around every newsstand in sight. No difference between breaking news and effective propaganda if it elicited the same glowering response from gruff, well-dressed, well-educated men.

Carden scarfed down the rest of his salted croissant while his mind digested the implications. For three months now he made it a habit to walk Marelyn to the Stock Exchange on Excelsior Avenue before work. The simple act oddly mollified the worst of the horribly-unfair accusations Marelyn suffered from her parents—especially ever-present Charles. Hypocritical of them since Carden still always left Marelyn to her own devices for the rest of the day. But it worked, Marelyn also grew oddly more pleasant these days, and it gave Carden and Marelyn a wonderful scheduled routine to catch news together, such as today.

Finally, he said after the last piece settled in his stomach, “I’ll probably be long at work today.”

Marelyn quirked a sharp eyebrow at him. “Are you that worried?”

Did it show on his face?

“I am,” Carden said without delving too deep to it. “All the hawks in Parliament will be giddy, and giddy hawks typically bring a lot of unfortunate consequences.”

Marelyn gave a long sigh, stared at the newspaper headlines for a moment, and then her expression hardened. “I’ll go see my father at Parliament and then drop by the Admiralty to have tea with my brother Jeremy.”

That sounded bizarre. Marelyn still despised her now secretly-heretical family. She almost never visited them without Carden in-tow to deflect their nagging for her to convert. It was Carden’s turn to quirk an eyebrow at her. She gave a helpless shrug and smiled. “We need all the information we can, right?”

Sometimes it truly was a blessing to have someone so bright around.

Carden pecked Marelyn on the forehead. He said, “Thank you. Sorry for the trouble and stay safe. What would I do without you.”

Carden left, not sure why Marelyn lingered in place for a moment longer.

Commonwealth Exchequer’s sunlit lobby absolutely teemed with uniformed officials from Customs and Revenue Board, Treasury Board, and Trade Bureau. Their freneticism inflated Carden’s dread at today’s anticipated workload with equal proportion. Even the floor he worked on bustled with sharply-attired, nervous-looking corporate men. Carden entered Edward Cook’s office after knocking—and froze at the sight of his father Julian standing side-by-side-with Edward.

The two men regarded Edward’s floor-to-ceiling world-map painted across an entire wall. Edward wore his typical brown business-suit whereas Julian wore his distinctive rich-purple business-suit. Edward said to Julian, “Oakmoore Industries must be having a jolly good time with all these military-contracts coming its way. Are you swimming in money yet, Julian old chap? Ever considered retiring?”

Julian chuckled, oblivious it seemed to Carden’s presence by the door. “Please, Edward old friend. All I do I do for the God of War’s glory and Alfainia’s continued prosperity. There’s nothing in it for me except some spare change to persevere through this life a tad longer. Work is reward enough.”

Carden nearly gagged.

Edward craned back and roared with laughter. “Do be generous with some of that spare change then, old chap. I could always use some of it.”

Edward walked Julian to the door and Julian’s expression flickered at Carden. “Son, wonderful to see you’re finally here.”

Damn it, could the day get any worse. Carden stood at attention and gave his father the barest courtesy demanded. “Father.”

“You weren’t in your office, so I decided to wait at Edward’s.” Julian spoke with a broad smile even though his eyes glinted cold. “Wanted to go over some things with you. I hate when personal and professional lives intersect, but who can help it?”

Carden smiled but did not try to hide his wince. “Indeed, father.”

Julian gripped Carden’s shoulder and then patted it. “I’ll be in your office. Edward, don’t rush things on account of me. I’ve got nothing but time.”

*I wish I **could just head home **this very moment** citing illness**.*

Once Julian left, Edward beckoned Carden over to his newly-painted world-map on the wall. Edward pointed at Alfainia, the island-nation like a resplendent dark-blue star on the map’s upper-right corner offshore the colorful Nordic Continent. Edward’s voice took on a gravity completely absent earlier in Julian’s presence.

“Alfainia is only as strong as its Dominions.”

Edward’s hand swept across the wall-map. Alfainia’s overseas Dominions sparkled like lesser light-blue stars, a collection of spots and strips on the world’s six other colorful continents. Edward said, “And the Dominions look to Alfainia for leadership.”

“It just takes the Dominions questioning Alfainia’s leadership for our beloved Commonwealth to unravel.”

Everyone knew that. Where was Edward going with this?

Edward abruptly moved to his new, lacquered cedarwood desk and beckoned Carden along. The desk boasted Julian’s gauche taste in every stylistically carved edge and relief-icon. Carden rolled his eyes behind Edward’s back at the ‘donated’ furniture. If only Carden could pile his father with ruinous taxes and get away with it.

Edward gestured at the thick white scroll unfurled across his desk and Carden paused. The thing positively radiated divinity to Carden’s mystical senses. A *Scripture*, but from whom?

“Go ahead, touch it.” Edward tapped the corner and briefly winced. “A pile of these appeared in Parliament two nights ago and nothing, not even Magic, could divine how they arrived.”

*Oh no.*

The moment Carden placed a finger on the scroll, an invisible updraft gripped his consciousness and flung him out to some unfathomably vast, empty void. Completely different from the God of War’s crushing divinity, he’d felt this before. Back on the mainland. A flat, emotionless voice rumbled all-around him in Arcane Tongue like a gathering thunderstorm.

“*I **recognize no God except myself**.**  
**I recognize no government except m**y own.**  
I recognize no religion except my own.**  
**I **recognize no peace** **except **my own**.**  
I remind the people of Evania Island th**ese things** because they have gone astray.**  
There is no such thing as Alfainia.**  
There is no such thing as an Alfainian nation.**  
There is no such thing as an Alfainian people.**  
Only **deluded **children **who must be rebuked.**  
Dissolve your** so-called governments and** **submit to** **my anointed officials.**  
Or **be rebuked with the fire of Sacred War**.*”

Carden placed the Creator’s *Scripture* down with trembling hands. He never remembered gripping it with both hands. Carden turned to Edward and asked, “What was Parliament’s response?”

Edward smirked.

Carden plopped into the seat because the tiled floor wobbled beneath his feet.

“I’m glad you understand,” Edward said and offered Carden a cup of chilled tea.

It all came together. Carden said, “Alfainia cannot falter, or the Dominions may question the Commonwealth’s continued existence.”

Edward nodded gravely. “Good man. You catch on quick, like your father. I knew I could count on you.”

Edward slid an open file to Carden. “Cabinet has decided we must accelerate rearmament. For one, we cannot keep relying on Sarh’s aerial-fleets to cover gaps in our airspace. Parliament has agreed to raise taxes higher so we can build more aerial-fleets.”

*N**o.*

“Sir, with all due respect,” Carden said and paused to double-check his next words. “Everywhere I’ve been these past six months, society is struggling. Can’t we make up the budget gap with bonds? I’m sure enterprises benefiting from the current rearmament would be more than happy to buy bonds in the name of god and country.”

A thought struck Carden and he suppressed a giddy grin. “My father’s, for example.”

Edward shot Carden a level look. “Perhaps it’s because you were never formally schooled in economics, so let me enlighten you. In the past hundred-fifty years our government has only ever successfully sold war-bonds in wartime and at usurious interest rates. How would you propose then we pitch such bonds to people like your father in peacetime when they know they can just for the inevitable war to receive better yields? Or imagine yourself in my shoes, Mister Oakmoore. How would you convince Parliament to issue war-bonds at wartime interest-rates in peacetime?”

Carden sank into his seat. By the Father…

“I’m not sure Alfainia’s working and middle classes can afford yet more,” Carden said at last. “If they can even afford their current taxes right now.”

Edward’s expression remained impassive. The man poured himself some wine from a bottle whose label unashamedly advertised its prestige, limited-batch vintage.

“I suppose our countrymen have a choice to make, then.”

Edward gestured out the window, where the Imperial District’s many historic spires peeked over the Government District’s roofs. Public monuments to the many documented atrocities Imperium Divinus committed during its nine-year occupation of the island.

“Higher taxes or Imperial citizenship.”

How did things come to this?

“That’s all, Mister Oakmoore.”

What did Carden’s generation do to deserve living in such times?

“Dismissed.”

No rest for the wicked. Carden returned to his own office and Julian’s presence offered no promise of respite. Carden’s father practically bubbled like a child when Carden closed and *locked* the door. No room for error here.

Except Carden’s father did even one better. Julian slipped on a ring-shaped Magic Artifact with Shadow Magic powers, took Carden’s arm, and pulled him into the desk’s shadow as if some stairs magically manifested. Carden and Julian stood face-to-face in the pitch-black, depthless, formless Shadow World. High above their heads, the ‘ceiling’ shimmered with Carden’s office viewed from the floor.

“The Creator truly smiles upon us,” Julian said and the fact he spoke in Arcane Tongue indicated he wanted nothing but truth from Carden. “Alfainia’s sham of a government will have no choice but to double-down on this foolish endeavor of rearmament. We must encourage them to dig their graves faster. Eventually this country will snap under extractive government and revolution.”

Something inside Carden snapped first. His sky-blue Magic leaked out his body like wisps of smoke from a kindling fire.

“It sure must gall you,” Carden said in Arcane Tongue and scrunched his nose at his father’s rich-purple suit. “To betray the country and system that has made you so rich for the vague promises of faith.”

Julian’s face froze.

Carden pressed on, laced every word with Zeal Magic. “I wonder, what does the Creator say about biting the hand that feeds it?”

Julian’s eyes flickered an inner orange light. Now wisps of Magic the color of dusk rose out Julian’s body. “Careful, boy.”

Circumstances no longer granted Carden the luxury of caution.

“You and mother were left with nothing when Imperium Divinus abandoned you all here. You became model citizens and built a thriving business-empire from nothing by provisioning Alfainia’s military. Tell me, in the Father’s Arcane Tongue, you don’t owe your current accomplishments to Alfainia’s generosity to you. I dare you.”

Julian’s face contorted into a snarl and his orange Armaments Magic frothed out now like boiling water. It cloaked him from head-to-toe in a suit of glowing orange plate-armor like a true elite Legionary of the Imperium.

“Take that back, Carden.”

When would Julian ever learn Carden never cared Julian stood a whole Tier above him as an Arcanist? If only Julian internalized why Carden’s Zeal Magic always made the man’s sorry heart squirm.

“No.” Carden’s Magical aura billowed out as a sky-blue conflagration. “How can you be so blinded by the Creator’s lies, when Arcane Tongue won’t even let you say Arcane Tongue is the Creator’s?”

“Carden!” Julian’s hand snapped forward and in a flash an Imperial-style broadsword the color of dusk pressed itself against Carden’s neck.

Carden ignored the sharp prick on his skin. He already had enough of these games. Carden spoke every subsequent word clearly, deliberately, and in unvarnished Arcane Tongue.

“There is only one true God and He is the Father.”

Carden imagined how Julian’s face twitched in anguish behind that sealed Magical visor.

“I dare you to say the same about the Creator. Go on, I’m waiting.”

The formless, depthless, infinite-black Shadow World stayed deathly-still. No light except pale white rays from above and the radiance of Carden and Julian’s Magic. Like two opposing stars beneath a shimmering black ocean.

Carden didn’t need to wait any longer. Zeal Magic clearly ate away at Julian’s confidence from the way Julian’s broadsword trembled worse as seconds passed. Carden smacked the blade’s hilt and knocked it out Julian’s shaking hands. The sword shattered into scattered motes of orange light when it landed and Carden faced Julian down.

Julian still refused to dissolve his Magical full-plate armor and reveal his face.

“And yet you accuse me of biting the hand that feeds me,” Julian said at last, helm-visor still closed and opaque. “What would your beloved Father in Heaven say if He saw you now?”

Carden frowned and flashed his new wedding-ring.

“I considered all my filial duties to you and mother settled the moment you forced me to marry Marelyn against our wills.”

Carden then rolled up his sleeve, flashed his Black Hounds military-tattoo at Julian, and said in Arcane Tongue, “And all I have today, I have because I bled and suffered in the trenches as a soldier of Alfainia. Not because of your money.”

“I have always and will always oppose the Creator you so love.”

“I owe the Creator nothing. I owe you nothing.”

“I owe everything to my Father in Heaven.”

Only later when Julian stormed out and Carden’s blood finally calmed did he notice the blood on his shirt-collar. Damn, the cut went quite deep and blood still trickled. Julian really did mean to seriously harm Carden earlier. Guess Carden’s Zeal Magic rattled him too much to follow through.

That was too close. No use trying to hide it from the staff. Carden went to the infirmary and admitted he got into a minor scuffle with his father, hence the cut. No, he did not want to press charges, what did the bloke think? Carden was not that unfilial yet. A green-haired, green-eyed Elf slave finally came over, her hand glowed with leaf-green Mitotic Magic, and she closed Carden’s wound in one horribly itchy minute.

Carden came home that evening and many tax-exemption petitioners later to find Marelyn rigidly seated in her personal study. When they first moved in to his civil-servant apartments early in their marriage, Marelyn claimed the second bedroom typically reserved for the civil servant’s children as her safe space. Carden obliged without hesitation at the time since they neither wanted children nor wanted to suffer too much of each other’s company at home.

Yet today Marelyn stared at her safe space’s collection of luxury purses that took up an entire wall with puffy red eyes. Carden ignored those eyes and the dried trails of snot that marred her lips and chin. He soaked a warm towel and only when he dabbed Marelyn’s face did she finally react.

Marelyn yanked Carden into an embrace.

Carden tried his best to brace his weight over Marelyn’s lap to not burden her. Throughout it all, Marelyn buried her face in his shoulder and said, “Everyone’s gone mad.”

For some odd reason, those words dragged out memories of corpses blasted to pieces over endless black fields churned by artillery-fire. Worse, the gut-churning nostalgia of that particular battle came back. Carden pressed Marelyn closer and stroked her back as much to steady himself as comfort her. Whatever Marelyn suffered was horribly contagious, he needed to get a grip. 

Marelyn continued on oblivious it seemed to Carden’s discomfort. “Not just my family, Carden. Government, the Admiralty, the old nobility. They’ve all gone mad.”

“Everyone wants war.”

As much as Carden’s thighs ached from holding his full weight back from Marelyn’s lap, he forced himself in that position for an unbearably long time. Marelyn pressed tighter into his shoulder and for the life of him he could not fathom a safe response to make her budge. So they embraced each other in Marelyn’s study that way, Carden perched awkwardly on Marelyn’s lap, his hand constantly stroking her back, until his thighs lamented and the evening chill seeped in. Only when Marelyn’s stomach rumbled a little too loud did she finally regain a little bit of life and Carden inwardly screamed in relief.

Carden made a scratch-stew of sliced pork sausages, carrots, onions, and potatoes. As they ate, Marelyn recounted everything she learned from her father Charles in Parliament, brother Jeremy in the Admiralty, the officers’ wives in the Admiralty’s social club, and her married female friends from the old nobility.

“War.” Carden repeated the word at the end of Marelyn’s recollection. His gut roiled and even the food tasted bitter now.

“And I can already see it,” Marelyn said as she scraped her bowl clean. “There will be nothing left for us except debt and misery. The Stock Exchange already reflected that when I dropped by before it closed earlier. Every sector is hemorrhaging except war-related industries, and that tells you everything where the money for that boom is coming from.”

Carden tried to forget his conversation with Edward and Julian, but Marelyn deserved to know everything. So Carden shared all he experienced today, down to the smallest detail. Marelyn shook her head when Carden repeated his conversation with Edward about preserving Alfainia’s leadership in the Commonwealth and rearmament. Marelyn then clenched her utensils when Carden mentioned Julian’s threat with a sword. But by the time Carden finished his recollection, Marelyn covered her face with her hands and laughed bitterly. She said after a moment, “I must be the one mad. Here we are, the whole world falling apart, and I am married into the family benefiting from it all and hating every moment of it.”

“What do you think about all this, Carden?” Marelyn asked with her face still covered.

Carden shut his eyes because his right eyelid twitched relentlessly.

“We are a truly unfortunate generation. Both the cogs and victims of the wheels of history. The ones who must suffer so the Creator and God of War can finally settle their centuries-old feud. And worst of all, the ones who suffer most joyously because we’ve been so raptured believing we are stewards and heirs to some greater cause only the gods know.”

That came out a lot longer than Carden intended, but after everything he saw as a soldier and now as a career-bureaucrat, how could one better describe the absurdities around him?

“And yet…” Marelyn’s hands fell from her face and her glistening green-eyes abruptly snagged something in Carden.

“I feel like I’m exactly where I need to be.”

Carden blinked dumbly, spoon halfway to his mouth. Surely he misheard. The days events must have addled Marelyn’s sense of judgment. Carden lowered his spoon and cleared his throat. “I beg your pardon?”

Maybe Carden was also going mad.

Marelyn shook her head and abruptly reached across the dining table and cradled Carden’s right hand in both of hers. She rubbed Carden’s Black Hounds tattoo with her thumb while her warm wedding-ring pressed into Carden’s palm.

She said after a moment just cupping Carden’s hand, “My family proudly embraces heresy, my in-laws grow rich undermining the source of their wealth, and my friends are almost all deluded that war will reward their husbands with fast promotions rather than coffins.”

“Yet somehow as the Father would have it, I’m stuck with one of the rarest breeds of people left in our country.”

Marelyn said nothing else.

*Is she **insinuating that** I’m even worse than all of **them combined**?*

Carden forced out a nervous chuckle. “I hope you’ll bear with me then. Sorry that I’ve done nothing but heap problems on your life. If it’s any comfort, I’ve always believed you deserved a lot better.”

Of course he did not imply that to mean indulging her expensive shopping habits even more.

Marelyn laughed at that and released Carden’s hand.

“Carden, dear.” Marelyn’s eyes sparkled at him.

What in the Father’s name? He’d never heard those two words together from Marelyn’s mouth without some weight of dread in the air. Maybe Carden and Marelyn really had gone mad.

Marelyn rose, circled the table, and snaked her arms around Carden’s shoulders from behind. She pressed her cheek against his and he could not help but notice how refreshing her normal lavender perfume smelled. Her soft breaths down his collar tickled his collarbone.

“I have been horribly unfair to you. I’m sorry.”

*I’ve really gone mad.*

Before Carden could so much as speak, Marelyn squeezed warmly and said, “You’ve done nothing but given me exactly what I deserved.”

Carden placed his hands on Marelyn’s arms and squeezed. Not sure what compelled him to, but it just felt… right. Marelyn gave one last squeeze herself and then released her hold. She poured them both glasses of wine and offered a toast.

“Whatever comes tomorrow, let’s face it together. Promise?”

Every fiber in Carden blared a moment like this only came once in a lifetime. He lifted his glass and clinked Marelyn’s before the magnitude of their words and gestures sank in.

“Promise.”

But he never regretted the premature vow. Because a few days later Parliament hiked taxes again as Edward promised. More Imperial incursions then troubled Alfainia from north to south. Spring’s retreat before summer’s creeping heat mirrored Alfainia’s gradual erosion of certainty. Three months after Carden and Marelyn made their private vow, one of Alfainia’s Dominions reported the first ever Imperial airship incursions into a Dominion. Then another Dominion reported the same. And then another. And another. Until finally it dawned to even the most obstinate Alfainian citizen that the Commonwealth itself lay under undeclared siege.

A Commonwealth that demanded Alfainia mobilize to its defense.

A mobilization that unfortunately compounded Carden’s workload. He drowned under petitioners desperate for tax-relief. Then one day that started as mundane as any other, summer swept in sweltering heat and with it Annie and Roger back to Carden’s office.

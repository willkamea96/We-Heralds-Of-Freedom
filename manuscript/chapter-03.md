# Chapter Three – Inexorable Machinery Of Empire


*“PRIORITY TELEGRAM.”*

*“To: Marelyn **Winston** at** **24 Veronica Lane, Building B3, Room 334**, Portsmouth, Vastnir** Province, PO**22165.”*

*“**I am s**afe. Windbury** law and order** temporarily restored. Will **take Sunday **4 PM** train home.**  
Will check on our families and bring **you **word back in person.**  
Stay safe.**”*

*“**– **Carden Oakmoore.”*

Carden passed the telegraph officer a brass-colored ten-*cone* bill. The young woman deposited his payment in the mechanical cash-register and said with a heavy face, “The Government District Telegram Office will dispatch your message to Miss Marelyn Winston with the next available courier.”

Carden could not blame the woman for her long face. Praiseworthy enough she showed up for work after everything that happened earlier in the day.

Carden tipped his hat. “Thank you. Father bless you this Deliverance Day, young madam.”

Carden strode past the rifle-armed soldiers outside Central Square’s telegram office and paced briskly to Windbury Cathedral. He lit a cigar, took a deep puff, and ignored the rows of black-shrouded corpses that filled the square’s perimeter. All incessantly picked by criminal forensics personnel like scavengers hungry for more.

The grey skies above looked ready to weep at the sight. Things went too far. Carden’s stomach roiled from the stench of smokeless powder no matter how much sweet cigar-smoke he huffed.

What a wretched homecoming.

The remains of Windbury Cathedral’s soup-kitchen still lay scattered across the steps as he hurried up. More rifle-armed soldiers saluted Carden as he barreled past the cathedral’s imposing double-doors. As those soldiers saluted, the black hound insignias on their wrists practically snarled at Carden.

Inside the dank, candlelit building a mass of Windbury’s more affluent families packed the pews and overflowed to the vestibule and side hallways. A mixture of weeping, shivering, and deathly stillness which seeped ice-cold into Carden’s bones. Made worse by the fact he recognized and grew up with quite a few of the faces here.

“They killed… they killed Orwell.” Annie gibbered between sobs into Roger’s chest while Roger stroked her back. Carden’s body froze half-step, locked up at the prospect of Annie’s accusations. Martial law went too far. Why was his old unit, the Black Hounds, so conveniently here in Windbury armed and ready the same day a riot broke out?

*Horace*.

Speak of the Profane. The double-doors creaked wider and Horace in military attire stamped in with a platoon of soldiers in tow. Carden decided it was easier to face Horace than Annie in the moment. At least in that order of confrontation, he could seek answers for Annie’s closure.

If she even let him.

Carden made a gesture with his hand at Roger and pointed with his eyes at Horace. Roger nodded and surreptitiously ushered Annie and their kids deeper into the cathedral. Away from Carden and Horace.

“Comb this whole place, they’re here, I can feel it.” Horace snapped his fingers and the cathedral’s refugees reacted to the sound as if they heard stray gunfire. Horace’s eyes scrunched when they met Carden’s. “Carden?”

It took everything not to throttle Horace here in front of all these soldiers and poor citizens.

“Mayor Livingstone.” Carden snapped a quick salute. “A word in private?”

Horace’s face darkened and Aether briefly rippled. Thankfully the man relaxed a hair and said, “Of course.”

“You weren’t elected as mayor, were you?”

Horace scoffed, took another huff of his cigar, and stared down from their perch on the cathedral’s high balcony. Every facet of Windbury from here looked solemn and grey. Carden gripped the railing harder and asked, “Well?”

“I was elected,” Horace said again. “Parliament was just extremely generous with my campaign funds and my rivals conveniently ran into all sorts of legal issues.”

By the Father…

“This is happening all over the country, isn’t it?”

Horace said nothing but looked on with hooded eyes at his bloody handiwork strewn across Central Square below.

“Let’s head back downstairs, old friend.” Horace pushed off the railing and slinked into the cathedral’s dark interior. “My Law Magic just confirmed we found our suspects.”

The same auburn-haired, half-Elf young woman Carden encountered earlier struggled against the two burly soldiers that dragged her forward. She no longer wore her bright-scarves and flashy burgundy coat or wore her hair lose, but rather a drab and forgettable cotton dress-coat with her characteristic hair under a cap. Behind them, soldiers dragged along a struggling auburn-haired, middle-aged Human man and a despondent green-haired, grey-skinned Elf woman.

Soldiers forced all three suspects down on their knees before Horace. Aether stirred and when Horace spoke the way Law Magic infused his voice rattled Carden.

“*Confess*.” Horace’s eyes flickered an inner sky-blue light as he uttered that one word in Arcane Tongue. The three suspects visibly shuddered. Horace spoke next in regular vernacular.

“Did you instigate the riots?”

“Yes.”

“We did.”

Yet, when the auburn-haired man and green-haired Elf woman uttered those words, they writhed, wheezed, and bent over retching air. Horace shook his head and said in a kinder tone, “Don’t lie.”

The couple glanced at one another. The Elf woman’s long silk sleeves briefly pulled up and flashed the slave-tattoo on her right wrist. The pieces finally fell into place.

“We will not harm your daughter,” Carden said.

Carden ignored the dark look Horace shot him and pressed on. “Confess the truth if you so desire the law’s fair judgment.”

The Human man and Elf woman trembled and then bowed their heads. 

“No.”

At last, the half-Elf woman raised her head and met Carden’s gaze.

“Yes.”

The young woman seemingly suffered no backlash from Horace’s Law Magic, unlike her parents. But rather she stared resolutely at her potential executioners. That said everything.

Horace nodded towards the door and soldiers dragged the slack young woman out the cathedral. No faint gunfire that accompanied execution done out-of-sight. Just dread silence as Horace ordered the parents released and brushed their pestering questions off with empty thanks for cooperation.

Horace gripped Carden’s arm on the way out and whispered, “Next time, *don’t* challenge my authority like that again.”

Horace and his soldiers vanished as quickly as they appeared.

“What will happen to her?” Roger asked while Annie dozed like a sleeping angel on his arm. Carden averted his eyes from the sight.

“The Black Hounds specialize in pacifying occupied enemy settlements. They won’t let such a talented Ideals Arcanist go to waste. Especially with what’s coming.”

Roger pursed his lips but said nothing more.

Several dozen of Carden’s old high-school classmates came by and joined their circle in the cathedral’s emptier confession hallway. As the sun set outside, Carden caught up with and heard all sorts of stories. How some classmates struck gold and made it rich. How many drifted as outwardly middle-class but barely made ends meet. How the better-off ones joined Annie and Roger and conducted charity through their own enterprises.

How everyone suffered under Terence Howell’s crippling taxes.

“Did you also kill people today?” Annie and Roger’s teenage daughter abruptly asked. She peered at Carden with eyes too painfully like her mother’s.

She deserved the truth.

“I did.”

As Carden’s old classmates either recoiled or leaned closer, Carden lifted three fingers up. “The man who tried to stab your father while we were running here. An old woman who barged out a door swinging a hammer while I passed by. And…”

Carden recalled the slack wrist with a snarling black hound tattoo on a growing pool of blood.

“A soldier who I caught violating the law.”

Roger flinched. “Did anyone—”

Carden glared and Roger shut his mouth. Carden glared at his classmates next and they all shrank back. Enough said.

Carden butted the back of his head against the cathedral’s cold granite wall.

“Let’s just make it through the night.”

Barely half-an-hour later, air-raid sirens blared outside. The grim faces around Carden fell to a new low. Carden and the rest rushed to the nave where soldiers herded panicked refugees into the cathedral.

“Into the bomb-shelters, now! Imperial air-raid.”

Carden’s heart dropped to his feet. Visions of black airships high in the sky reared and the thunder of their barrages roared. Carden shook his head, exchanged a look with Roger, and both shepherded their group to the reinforced bomb-shelter underneath the cathedral’s crypts. When both men reemerged into the nave they stumbled upon Horace and an officer who wore regimental commander chevrons. Carden and Roger hurried over to the arguing men. The regimental commander glared at the two but Carden and Roger flashed their wrist insignias. The officer faltered and Horace swiped his hand. “They’re with me, commander.”

Horace gestured up and said, “Let’s get a better look.”

As they clambered to the top of Windbury Cathedral’s bell-tower, the tallest point in all Windbury, a droning buzz grew louder. Carden asked if it was safe and the signals-officer assured him those were friendly planes. Nearby AAF Gloucaster just scrambled multiple fighter-squadrons to cover Windbury’s airspace.

Carden and the rest crested the bell-tower’s stairwell and beheld the expansive dusky sky between the tower’s arches. They heard the droning turboprop-planes first before they saw them, four-plane squadrons high above Windbury like circling birds. The regimental commander pointed east at the dark sea many kilometers away, still glittering with dusk’s last rays. And there silhouetted against the evening sky, a cluster of tiny, sleek black shapes hovered over the horizon.

“Confirmed Imperial colors, maintaining distance from shore,” one of the officers said while he peered through long binoculars and the signals-officer repeated those words into a radio’s mouthpiece. “Two *Dominus*-class supercarriers, five *Princep*-class heavy-cruisers, thirty-three frigates and destroyers classes unidentifiable.”

Carden turned to Horace. The man shrugged and said without meeting Carden’s gaze, “You didn’t think government mobilized army just for riots, did you?”

Carden’s eyes widened.

“Are we officially at war?” Roger asked.

The regimental commander scoffed. “Officially, no. But you know how things are.”

Right. Carden’s deployments on the mainland were never wars either. Humanitarian interventions or special military operations.

Peals of thunder clapped them from behind. Carden and everyone else ducked into a crouch with heads between knees and hands clasped behind necks. When nothing happened, they rose and found metallic vessels of various sizes cruising silently over Windbury towards the Imperial flotilla. Every hull emblazoned with Sarh’s flags and the God of War’s iconography. Behind them, even more sleek vessels popped into existence with the characteristic rumbling thuds of Space Magic’s teleportation.

A male voice crackled out the radio.

“This is Commander Shapur Mihran of the Sarhese Eighteenth Aerial Fleet. Moving to shadow Imperial flotilla.”

The Sarhese fleet sped out to sea on their Gravitational Magic engines like silent specters of war, flanked by squadron after squadron of Alfainian fighter-planes. Meanwhile out at sea, dark storm clouds billowed to life over the imperial flotilla. With supernatural speed those distant vessels and the entire horizon vanished behind impenetrable sheets of black rain.

Carden admired the Sarhese commander’s caution of halting his fleet’s advance and hugging the coastline instead. No point tempting a storm the Imperial flotilla clearly conjured with Atmospheric Magic for cover. Besides, in Carden’s experience, Atmospheric Magic of that scale screamed the work of Imperial Demigods.

Thankfully nothing else happened and the storms dissipated in the dead of night. No imperial vessels in its wake, just eerie wind and silence and a city’s priorly-riotous population confined to bomb-shelters all night.

The next morning, Carden helped his various friends home and then stopped by the telegram office. He half-expected a priority telegram from Edward Cook or someone else in government after everything that transpired. As much as the prospect of more work clenched his gut, the censure of neglecting his duties roiled it much worse. Besides, that gave a wonderful excuse later if his parents asked why he didn’t visit right away.

*“PRIORITY TELEGRAM.”*

*“To: Carden Oakmoore, Special Commissioner of Income Tax, **Windbury Central Telegram Office**, **Ostnir Province**, PO2**4000**.”*

*“I am coming on **the **first** available** **Sunday **train. **Please wait **for me.”*

*“– Marelyn Winston.”*

Carden’s jaw dropped. The telegram came last night.

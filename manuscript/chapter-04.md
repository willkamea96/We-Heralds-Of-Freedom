# Chapter Four – Great Vagaries Of Homecoming


Marelyn disembarked the near-empty train clad in comfortable brown trousers, a loose beige blouse, and the blue-purse Horace bought slung on her shoulder. Her strawberry-blonde hair showed traces of stubborn bed-hair that betrayed a night of fitful sleep. Her half-focused green eyes froze when Carden met them from the platform.

“You actually waited.”

Carden fought the twitch in his eye. He said with a genial smile, “It would’ve been impossible for you to track me down amidst this madness.” 

*Drama is the last thing I want right now.*

Marelyn blinked. A couple of soldiers nearby glanced curiously at her.

Carden cleared his throat and extended his hand in an offer to carry her purse. He blinked when Marelyn slipped her *hand* into his instead, clasped tightly, and nudged him after her towards the station’s exit. What in the Father’s name was happening today…

Marelyn jerked to a stop and swiveled her head around Central Square. Her eyes blinked dumbly at the handful of black-shrouded corpses still unclaimed by next-of-kin. Looked like soldiers already started packing some up into hearse-carriages for cremation. Marelyn’s hand went slack in Carden’s.

“Not here,” Carden said, pointed with his eyes at a nearby military squad on patrol, and tugged Marelyn towards one of the few open cafés. “I’ll fill you in once we’re somewhere private. It’s a mess.”

Marelyn’s face blanched the more she heard until her paleness plateaued somewhere halfway into Carden’s recollection. When the silence dragged a little too long and she refused to stir, Carden nudged Marelyn’s plate of now-cold scones against her rigid arm.

“Come eat.”

Marelyn finally broke out of her reverie. “How can you be so calm about all this?”

Carden blinked. Was he?

“I honestly don’t know.” Carden sighed and took another deep sip of the extremely bitter coffee. “Maybe it just hasn’t caught up to me, or maybe I already saw too much in the army.”

Outside, a worried-looking mother hurried her curious child along when the boy stopped to stare at two off-duty soldiers laughing over a smoke.

“What brought you to Windbury?” Carden asked in the hopes they moved on to lighter topics.

Marelyn’s lip opened and closed as if she struggled to speak. Even her normally inscrutable green eyes blinked rapidly.

“I was worried.”

*Fair point, her family’s in Windbury too, no matter how long-estranged they’ve been.*

Carden sighed and stared at the ceiling-fan’s looping, pointless revolutions. He felt the exact same way at the prospect of visiting his old home and parents. Nothing to it.

“I’m happy you came,” Carden said and for some odd reason Marelyn flinched. “Truth is, I’ve been dreading visiting our parents. Never imagined you being here actually helped me find the nerve. Thanks for having my back.”

Marelyn suddenly clasped her hands over her lap and hid a smile.

“You’re very welcome.”

What an odd day.

During the carriage-ride to the Oakmoore estate, Carden took a deep breath and then gave Marelyn a grim look. Her eyes sharpened instantly.

“There’s something you need to know about my parents. They were Imperial colonists who chose to stay after the liberation. But they’ve never abandoned their faith, and so…”

Carden finished his compressed version of the Oakmoore family’s history he owed Marelyn twenty-five years ago right as the carriage stopped in front of a wide, latticed brass gate. Behind it his family’s expansive marble manor rose amidst vast manicured lawns, flowering hedges, and massive topiaries of fierce Aetheric Creatures. If only they came under happier circumstances so Carden could show Marelyn the Imperial iconography his parents subtly layered into the architecture.

Marelyn cupped her cheek and gaped wordlessly the whole while, and now her bright-green eyes alternated between the manor and Carden. Yet when Carden reached out to reassure her, she inhaled deeply and flashed him a resolute look.

“So all this time my in-laws were secret-believers of the Creator and potential Imperial collaborators.”

Marelyn’s voice sounded so matter-of-fact and her face so placid Carden wondered which of them just confessed the damning generational secret.

Marelyn nodded at the manor and asked, “Shall we?”

Carden’s heart skipped a beat. Opening the gates and knocking on the manor’s grand double-doors felt normal by comparison.

“So the prodigal son returns.”

Carden’s father, Julian, glared at Carden and Marelyn with dark eyes more severe than Carden remembered. He sat on that high-backed chair shaped like a throne and judged Carden and Marelyn from behind the study’s wide, lacquered teakwood desk. Marelyn maintained her demure and proper posture on the reception room’s gold-rimmed velvet sofa. Carden sat next to her, balanced on the sofa’s wooden edge, and refused to yield before his father’s gaze.

“Six years since you’re last visit, still no grandchild, son?” Julian’s severe gaze turned from Carden to Marelyn. “Has bright little Marelyn finally found a mystery she can’t solve?”

From the corner of Carden’s eye Marelyn gave a courteous smile that didn’t reach her eyes. “Carden and I both understand exactly where our respective talents lie.”

Carden’s mother, Jane, scoffed at Marelyn from the plush loveseat and then fanned her deeply-creased face with a paper fan. “So you still fancy yourself a better investor than all those brilliant men who spend their whole lives mastering the science?”

Jane wrapped her rich crimson shawl tighter around herself. “Perhaps, Marelyn, if you’d devoted more time to Carden’s needs and less to the approbation of other married men, you’d have something to show for all your efforts.”

Marelyn opened her mouth but Jane pointed at Marelyn’s indigo purse. “Don’t think I never hear what you do behind my son’s back whenever I visit Portsmouth.”

Marelyn’s smile grew wider yet her wrist’s tendons bulged as she clenched her purse’s strap.

Not good!

Carden placed his hand over Marelyn’s, terrifyingly hot to the touch.

“*I* do not want children, and neither does Marelyn. We are united in this and I will hear no more. Are we clear? And Marelyn’s done nothing but reward my trust in the past twenty-five years she’s managed our money. I daresay if I had my portfolio here right now, you’d see Marelyn’s done better than any three of my older brothers combined.”

Julian and Jane stared at Carden as if he just uttered the most absurd statement. Marelyn’s hands cooled from burning hot to steady warmth. For some odd reason Marelyn regarded him with a strangely curious look.

*Unless I’m horribly mistaken about our finances**. I just take **Marelyn’s **word we’re doing** well**.*

Julian grated his seat’s wooden legs against the floor and rose. Carden’s father strode to the floor-to-ceiling bookcase, adjusted his crimson wool robe, and tapped one of the shelves in a particular sequence. The bookcase retracted in half into the walls and revealed the hidden alcove with the Creator’s unmistakable icon. A golden statue of a leafless, eighteen-branched tree that symbolized the Arcane’s Eighteen Magic Branches on a pure-white marble pedestal. And beneath the pedestal stacks of documents and golden Imperial coins.

Carden silently thanked the Father and His Son that he confessed the Oakmoore family secret to Marelyn earlier. He barely restrained his surprise yet Marelyn admirably kept up her characteristic inscrutable expression. Looked like his parents suspected nothing, though he yearned to ask why they so brazenly showed this in front of Marelyn. The Winstons knew nothing about—

“While you two have been busy making pocket change, your mother and I finally won the Winstons over to our cause.” Julian took out some documents, resealed the secret alcove, and laid them on his desk. “They are convinced—no, convicted just as we are, that naught but ruin awaits Alfainia until it reembraces the one true faith. They eagerly yearn for Alfainia’s return to the Creator’s empire.”

Marelyn’s fingers surreptitiously laced between Carden’s and clenched his hand so hard her knuckles turned white, yet her face remained implacable if outwardly genial as always. Carden instantly understood what she wanted. 

“You won the Winstons over? When?”

Jane uttered a hoarse laugh and slapped her folded paper fan over the loveseat’s arm with a *crack*. “You know how these things go, dear child. There’s no dramatic moment where the heart suddenly flips. Your father and I just lathered it little by little for twenty-five years until one day poof, the Winstons begged us for the Lord’s truth.”

Carden prodded on and asked the questions Marelyn could not bear to ask. Jane and Julian happily obliged over the next hour how they converted every member of Marelyn’s family to the Creator’s faith.

“Best investment I ever made,” Julian said with a wolfish grin and his dark eyes undeniably gleamed at Marelyn’s fat-diamond wedding-ring. “Marelyn’s father finally won one of Windbury’s Parliamentary seats riding on Justice. Marelyn’s brother Jeremy just got promoted to Rear Admiral this year. And after at last that pharmaceutical company Marelyn’s sister Eleanor runs with her husband Gladwell has its lucky break with all these new long-term military contracts. I’m sure you understand what that means for us right, Carden?”

Carden fought hard not to retch. He squeezed Marelyn’s hand back with equal need.

Julian sank back to his seat with a contented sigh, done perusing the clandestine documents from earlier. “It took far longer than I expected and an obscene amount of money, but the Creator has truly rewarded my faithfulness. What else could yesterday’s Imperial raid have been if not a sign from the Creator Himself?”

Carden almost blurted out he was happy for his parents so he and Marelyn could just leave, but Marelyn abruptly spoke up in a trembling voice.

“So you laced your poison into my family when they were at rock-bottom? And you call that real faith?”

The study plunged into frigid silence. Carden’s heart stopped.

Julian’s face reddened in that tell-tale sign of explosive rage yet before he blew up Jane scurried over. The tall lithe woman rubbed Julian’s shoulders until his expression simmered down to a dark glare. All the while Jane spoke with an ugly scowl.

“If you are as bright as you think you are, why couldn’t you flip your family’s fortunes around yourself, little Marelyn? Why did your parents so eagerly marry you and not one of your many marriageable sisters off, hmmm?”

Carden wished he could just leave and let Marelyn suffer the consequences of her blithe mouth, but her grip on his hand hardened. Marelyn opened her mouth but Julian suddenly spoke over her.

“In fact, that’s a lovely idea. Go pay your parents a visit, Marelyn, I insist. They must surely be suffering because of my coercion. Do free them of the Creator’s dreadful yoke.”

Marelyn’s lips trembled. “Perhaps I shall.”

As Carden walked Marelyn out, Julian called out behind them. “Oh and Carden, one more thing. Good job squeezing the people with all these taxes. Make them see this is the only reward they’ll ever receive for following the Father and God of War.”

Carden paused at the doorway. Marelyn’s shimmering green eyes blinked back tears. She pressed their pressed hands against her trembling hip.

Damn it.

Carden pulled his sleeve back and flashed his Black Hound tattoo to Jane and Julian.

“You know exactly where my loyalties have always been.”

Julian’s boisterous laughter hounded Carden’s steps out. “Sure doesn’t seem that way, son.”

Thankfully Marelyn spoke nothing during the carriage-ride to her parents’ place. Just buried her face in Carden’s arm and gave him a measure of blessed peace. He stroked her lap, not sure what else she wanted from him to stay serene.

When their carriage pulled up to the Winston’s, a tall modernist manor in downtown Windbury only fifteen years old, Marelyn silently slid out the carriage. Carden hurried after his wife’s brisk steps.

Charles and Beatrice Winston looked far younger than Carden’s parents even though both were technically older. Both elders sported heads of rich blonde-hair, their pale green eyes sparkled brightly, and they fussed over Carden and Marelyn with vim. A cheerfulness Marelyn clearly did not expect from how she constantly blinked at her parents.

“I daresay,” Charles said and cupped Marelyn’s hands, “it’s so good to see you, Marelyn dear. You never visit!”

Beatrice came over with a platter of tea and pushed warm teacups into Carden and Marelyn’s hands. “Carden, you’re finally gaining weight! I’m so happy Marelyn’s finally learned how to feed you properly.”

Carden’s tongue stuttered until he finally blurted the first coherent thought. “Marelyn is a blessing. She understands me perfectly. By the way, father, congratulations! You won your seat in Parliament, long overdue if I do say so myself.”

“Thank you, young chap!” Charles slapped his thigh, beamed at a Marelyn who sat stiffer by the second, and gestured at Carden and Marelyn seated together. “See? Color me impressed. Twenty-five years, Marelyn. Look how you made it work.”

A tendon bulged in Marelyn’s neck. Carden squeezed Marelyn’s hand and hoped it averted the outburst he knew was coming. Marelyn inhaled deeply but ultimately held her breath.

“Mother, father,” Marelyn said in the flattest of tones. “I heard there was an air-raid yesterday. I came on the first train. Hope you both were okay.”

Beatrice’s eyes widened. She dropped her platter with a clang, gripped Marelyn’s shoulders, and nattered with the widest of smiles. “Marelyn, I can’t tell you how excited we all were. The Creator Himself has seen how we’re suffering under the God of War’s boot. What else was that show-of-force except His promise of relief? No more of these wretched taxes!”

Marelyn’s face paled precipitously. Charles chattered on oblivious to his daughter’s change.

“Marrying you to Carden truly was our best decision. Julian and Jane have been so helpful despite their commoner origins and they taught me so much. I daresay, our family was too stuck in the old ways. Maybe that’s why we ran into such hard times back then.”

“Father, how could you—”

Carden snatched Marelyn’s hand and squeezed it against his lap. He spoke over her. “Father, I’m curious. You’ve been part of the old nobility your whole life. You’re practically family with Alfainia’s founders. What do you think about all this?”

Charles sighed and stared at the statue of sword-raising Saint Alfred on the fireplace mantle. “We were once a great country, Carden. Saint Alfred freed us from the old Imperium’s rot more than a hundred years ago and we named our glorious nation after him. Yet, look at us now. Things have been downhill since the occupation and liberation, what, fifty, sixty years ago? Yet the Creator’s Imperium grows stronger by the day.”

Carden held back his remark that Charles should not blanket the past fifty-four years as uninterrupted decline considering the statistics, especially as a member of Parliament. But he needed the man to keep talking so Marelyn internalized reality.

“I think we’ve lost our way. The Imperium’s old rot is clearly gone. They couldn’t have possibly enjoyed such sustained resurgence otherwise.”

Carden drew the line there. He plastered on a smile but edged his words in iron.

“Father, you sure have an interesting definition of resurgence. I was in the army twenty-five years ago and keep in touch with friends still in the army. The Imperium’s cannibalizing its future for this last lunge at worldwide hegemony, you know that right?”

“Bah!” Charles swatted Carden’s remark aside. “If that were the case the God of War would not be so desperate mobilizing half the world for His war against the Creator. It’s *we* who are cannibalizing our future for a last lunge at being meaningful on the world stage.”

Marelyn’s jaw dropped.

“I appreciate your insight, Mister Winston.” Carden rose and beckoned a half-resisting Marelyn up after him. “You’ve enlightened us, truly. Sorry, but Marelyn and I do have to run. It’s a long train-ride back to Portsmouth.”

“Don’t be a stranger. I’ll much appreciate your counsel, Carden, when I start pushing for changes in Parliament.” Charles leaned forward and rubbed his hands together. “In fact, I’ll be in Portsmouth long-term soon. Marelyn, I can’t wait to drop by more often.”

Inexplicably Charles frowned and wagged a finger at Marelyn. “The Creator knows how worried I am about you, silly child. My friends at the capital keep telling me how awfully you neglect Carden. Why are you still galivanting like some mannish pleb playing stock-trader? When will you grow up?”

Marelyn’s hand turned ice-cold in Carden’s. She opened her mouth but Beatrice cut her off.

“Marelyn!” Beatrice gasped and covered her mouth. “We’ve been over this. I am sick of Jane nattering to me how you keep failing as a wife. You understand me, child? Be more like your sisters and start acting like a proper lady of old nobility.”

Marelyn’s mouth worked but no words came out. She blinked at her parents as if dazed. Finally, she tugged Carden to the door and never looked back.

“Sorry, mum, dad, but as my husband said, we do need to get going.”

Every part of Marelyn from eyelids to lips to arms trembled fiercely. Her green eyes glistened like pearls in the corridor’s shadow. Things couldn’t end like this. Carden needed to step in or all this pent-up wrath in her was going to hound *him* for Father knew how long.

Carden stopped, faced Beatrice and Charles, and said gravely, “Father, mother, with all due respect, apologize. Now.”

Oh no, Marelyn started blinking back tears. Did she not want an apology from her parents?

Carden hurriedly added in a graver tone. “For twenty-five years, Marelyn’s made no lapse of judgment that’s made me doubt her aptitudes. I meant every word earlier that Marelyn’s been a blessing in my life.”

Shoot, Marelyn stifled a sob. If only she just told him what he should tell her parents.

“Marelyn is perfect the way she is.”

Marelyn tugged Carden closer right as Charles roared with laughter and Beatrice spoke with a mirthful expression. “Please, Carden, you flatter us. But don’t try to brush aside Marelyn’s failings just to spare our feelings. If she ever gives you trouble, please let us know. We’ll happily help you straighten her out.”

Carden opened his mouth to rebuke but Marelyn abruptly dragged him out.

He’d never heard her slam a door shut that hard before.

Carden and Marelyn shuffled down the wide concrete road back to Windbury’s train-station in silence. Marelyn refused a coach and insisted she wanted a mindless walk. Except for the Winston Manor, every other shop, flat, and single-family residence they passed downtown showed various states of skipped maintenance. Marelyn drank it all in with the same strange luster in her green eyes ever since they left.

“Sorry about that.” Carden groped for words yet somehow could think of nothing more suitable. “Did you… did you want to do anything else while we’re in Windbury?”

Marelyn stared at him with an inscrutable expression. Of course.

*What a mess.*

Marelyn’s gaze dropped to her wedding ring and its fat diamond. If only she just said what was on her mind.

Without warning, Marelyn yanked her ring off and hurled it into a dark, trash-strewn alleyway. Carden stared after the ring and then at Marelyn’s emotionless face.

By the Father… was she finally divorcing him? Couldn’t blame her, honestly. Nothing in her life went right the moment she married him.

“How silly of me, I seem to have lost the wedding-ring your parents bought me.” Marelyn’s eyes sparkled at Carden. “Could we please stop by a shop and buy a new one?”

What in the bloody Heavens above and the Profane-infested hells below…

Oh.

Carden slipped his plain gold wedding-ring off and flicked it away. It clattered onto the road, rolled off, and fell into a gurgling roadside gutter.

“Funny, I seem to have lost the one your parents bought me too.”

Marelyn’s expression positively glowed.

Carden nodded towards another street. “One of my old classmates happens to be a jeweler. He loves making bespoke jewelry from the strangest things. Maybe he could give us a discount.”

For the first time since he married Marelyn, she smiled at him like the happiest little girl.

Of course, his old classmate refused to entertain any discount in such lean times. But Marelyn’s complexion absolutely brightened the more she described to the jeweler her envisioned design. The glow on Marelyn’s face at the final product made the gut-churning twelve-hundred-*cone* expense far more palatable. The rings admittedly glinted nicely under the light. Marelyn certainly couldn’t stop admiring hers.

Wedding-rings of plain, polished bands of gold and silver folded together to look like a pair of entwined snakes.

They stopped by Annie and Roger’s factory, Bakers For You, before going home. Carden made his decision on the Baker case and Marelyn wholeheartedly agreed. The soup-kitchen’s line wrapped around several blocks today. Many averted their gaze when Carden in his Commissioner uniform walked past and even Marelyn shot Carden a concerned look.

Roger froze behind the counter when he met Marelyn’s eyes. “Marelyn… Winston?”

Marelyn’s eyes wandered between Roger, Annie, and their four teenage children who mirrored their parents’ countenances.

“I was in the area and figured…”

Carden never heard Marelyn so lost for words over a simple greeting.

“… I missed your pastries.”

Roger blinked like a man stupefied. Annie shot Carden a quizzical, if borderline offended look. Carden gave a helpless smile and shrugged back.

“Why don’t we go inside?” Roger uttered his words with noticeable stiffness. “I remember you loved the hazelnut-cream croissants. We just made several fresh batches off the line.”

“That sounds sublime, but no thank you.” Marelyn clenched Carden’s hand with a desperate grip despite the broad smile she beamed Roger. “Carden and I won’t be long. Whatever you have here will do. We’ll pay.”

When Carden announced his approval of Roger’s case for tax-exemption, Annie and Roger jumped in joy. Their big family hugged and even the soup-kitchen’s many visitors applauded and hooted. Carden stood apart, courteously brushed off many who insisted on shaking his hands, and gave the Bakers their due berth for celebration.

Marelyn helped deflect some of the folk who came to Carden. But otherwise, she just leaned on his arm, cheek on shoulder, and watched the Bakers while nibbling away a day-old scone. Even Carden noticed the shallowness of her bites and breaths. 

“Carden,” Marelyn said during the train ride home as they both stared out at a sky turning to twilight.

“I’m sick of being sad.”

Marelyn clasped her hands on the cabin’s table as if in prayer.

“I think… I think I’m ready to be happy.”

While Marelyn looked away, Carden slipped out a wrapped hazelnut-cream croissant from his briefcase. One he asked Annie to snatch for him earlier while Roger and Annie’s four children distracted Marelyn. Carden nudged the croissant against Marelyn’s lips while she stared vacantly at the darkening scenery.

“Come eat.”

Marelyn took a nibble.

That first, tiny bite contorted Marelyn’s face through all sorts of expressions. Finally, she teared up and gobbled the whole thing down half-laughing, half-sobbing.

That night back in their Portsmouth flat, for the first time in however many years Carden dozed off in bed with Marelyn snoozing in his arms.
